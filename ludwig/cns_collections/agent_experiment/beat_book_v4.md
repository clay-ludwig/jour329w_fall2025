# **The Maryland Education Beat: A Guide for Early-Career Journalists**

Welcome to the education beat in Maryland, where a multi-billion-dollar reform effort, pandemic recovery challenges, and culture war battles are reshaping how students learn and teachers teach. This is a beat defined by money, politics, and the deeply human stories of kids and families navigating a system in flux.

## **Understanding the Landscape: The Blueprint Dominates Everything**

If you cover education in Maryland right now, you need to understand one thing above all else: the [Blueprint for Maryland's Future](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/03/04/democrats-love-marylands-education-blueprint-but-no-one-knows-how-to-pay-for-it/) is the sun around which everything else orbits. This ambitious $16.6 billion education reform plan, passed in 2021 after overriding then-Gov. Larry Hogan's veto, aims to overhaul Maryland's public schools through five pillars:

1. **Early Childhood Education**: Universal pre-K for all 3- and 4-year-olds
2. **High Quality and Diverse Teachers**: Better pay, training, and recruitment
3. **College and Career Readiness**: Career counselors in every high school, preparing students by 10th grade
4. **More Resources**: Increased funding for schools, especially in high-poverty areas
5. **Governance and Accountability**: A seven-member oversight board watching implementation

The problem? [Nobody knows how to pay for it long-term](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/03/04/democrats-love-marylands-education-blueprint-but-no-one-knows-how-to-pay-for-it/). The state's structural deficit is projected to hit $2.93 billion by fiscal year 2029, just as Blueprint costs balloon to over $4 billion annually. Gov. Wes Moore remains committed to the plan, but the funding crisis looms large.

**Key source**: Rachel Hise, executive director of the Accountability and Implementation Board, oversees Blueprint implementation statewide.

**Story angles to watch**:
- Budget battles in Annapolis as the structural deficit grows
- Which counties are thriving under the Blueprint and which are struggling
- The [tension between Blueprint mandates and local budget realities](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/05/07/unrealistic-and-unfunded-marylands-blueprint-for-education-has-counties-concerned/)
- The Blueprint oversight board's annual evaluations (watch for their reports on the 92 data measures they track)

**Caveat**: As of early 2025, Gov. Moore [proposed cuts to Blueprint funding](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/02/21/moores-blueprint-cuts-face-major-pushback/) amid Maryland's budget crisis, facing major pushback from educators and advocates. This is an ongoing story that may have evolved significantly since these stories were published.

## **The Crisis Stories: What's Broken and Who's Trying to Fix It**

### **Chronic Absenteeism: The Invisible Crisis**

Nearly [27% of Maryland students were chronically absent](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/02/27/thousands-of-maryland-students-are-chronically-absent-each-year-heres-what-we-know/) in the 2023-2024 school year—meaning they missed 10% or more of school days. Baltimore City had the worst rates, with nearly half of students chronically absent. Two Baltimore schools reported rates higher than 90%.

This isn't just a Maryland problem—it's pandemic-related and nationwide—but the impacts are severe: lower graduation rates, reduced college enrollment, and widening achievement gaps. Hispanic and Black students are disproportionately affected.

**Key people**: 
- Principal Yetunde Reeves at Paul Laurence Dunbar High School in Baltimore
- Jing Liu, assistant education professor at the University of Maryland
- Mary Gable, assistant state superintendent at MSDE

**Story ideas**:
- Follow-up on the chronic absenteeism task force bills introduced in January 2025
- Profile schools with innovative attendance strategies (like Excel Academy at Francis M. Wood High School, which increased daily attendance by 16%)
- Investigate transportation barriers in Baltimore City, where some students travel 90 minutes to school
- Explore the connection between school climate/safety and attendance

### **Child Care Collapse: The Foundation Is Crumbling**

Maryland lost [15% of its child care providers and nearly 7% of child care slots](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/06/25/maryland-sees-sharp-drop-in-child-care-availability-since-covid-19-pandemic/) between January 2020 and January 2024. Some counties were hit even harder—St. Mary's County lost more than a quarter of its providers.

This crisis undermines the Blueprint's pre-K expansion plans. The state is counting on private child care providers to deliver universal pre-K, but [nearly 40% are declining to participate](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/07/09/the-state-is-counting-on-child-care-providers-to-offer-pre-k-but-many-are-uninterested/), citing concerns about staffing requirements, state oversight, and whether they'll lose income once free pre-K becomes available.

The human cost is real: Parents on seven different waitlists for infant care. Families hiring unlicensed providers out of desperation. Parents leaving the workforce entirely.

**Key sources across counties**:
- Karen Gideon (Washington County - Star Brite daycare)
- Brittany Truitt (Wicomico County)
- Bridgette Lawrence (St. Mary's County)
- Elizabeth Greenwell (Kent County - Kinder Connection)

**Story angles**:
- The staffing crisis in child care (providers can't compete with public school salaries)
- Unlicensed day care operations emerging to fill gaps
- The disconnect between Blueprint pre-K mandates and provider willingness to participate
- Infant and toddler care shortages (most severe age group)
- How the end of federal pandemic relief in September 2023 affected providers

### **Mental Health: Too Much Need, Not Enough Resources**

Maryland made a bold move in recent years, launching a [statewide youth mental health program](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/02/24/maryland-budget-crunch-prompts-lawmakers-gov-moore-to-trim-youth-mental-health-effort/) that supported over 58,000 students in its first eight months. Then the budget crisis hit. Funding was initially slashed from $110 million to $40 million, though it was later partially restored to $70 million.

But there's innovation happening too. Anne Arundel County's [STAR Program](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/06/23/anne-arundel-schools-pioneer-rapid-response-program-for-teens-suffering-from-addiction/) connects students struggling with substance abuse directly to treatment through school nurses. About 50 students have been referred to services since its launch—not a huge number, but each one represents a potential life saved.

**Key sources**:
- Karen Siska-Creel, Anne Arundel County's school health and support director
- Ryan Voegtlin, assistant superintendent of student services at Anne Arundel County Public Schools
- Organizations receiving mental health grants in Baltimore and Prince George's counties (see the detailed grant recipient lists in the database)

**Story angles**:
- Track mental health funding cuts versus actual service delivery
- [Behavioral health screening tools](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/04/28/schools-can-screen-students-for-behavioral-health-issues-but-many-are-reluctant-to-do-so/) like BIMAS-2 that schools are reluctant to use
- School-based substance abuse programs in other counties
- The intersection of mental health and chronic absenteeism

### **Teacher Shortage: The Workforce Crisis**

Maryland faces a [significant teacher shortage](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/03/18/marylands-teacher-shortage-will-the-blueprints-plan-for-better-pay-training-do-enough/), with 28 subject areas lacking educators in recent years, up from 17 five years prior. The Blueprint promises better pay and training, but experts remain skeptical that these measures alone will solve recruitment and retention challenges.

**Story ideas**:
- Profile districts with innovative "Grow Your Own" programs (Harford County has a strong one)
- Investigate National Board Certification programs and whether districts are supporting teachers in pursuing them
- Compare teacher salaries across counties and track Blueprint salary requirement compliance
- Teacher pipeline programs at Maryland universities

## **The Culture War Battlegrounds**

### **Gender Identity and Parental Rights**

[Carroll County's policy](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/12/10/carroll-countys-transgender-policies-clash-with-maryland-department-of-education-guidelines/) requiring mandatory parental notification when students wish to change their pronouns directly contradicts state guidelines. This makes Carroll County uniquely stringent in Maryland on this issue.

The debate is fierce. Conservative groups like Moms for Liberty argue parents have a "fundamental right" to know everything about their children's school lives. Advocates for LGBTQ+ students argue these policies put vulnerable kids in danger by potentially outing them to unsupportive or abusive parents.

In the [2024 school board elections](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/11/08/md-conservatives-make-gains-in-school-board-races/), conservative candidates backed by the 1776 Project PAC won or led in 11 out of 19 races across Maryland, defying expectations in this blue state. School board races are increasingly politicized battlegrounds.

**Key sources**:
- Wendy Novak and Shaun Maidens, mothers of transgender students in Carroll County
- Kit Hart, Moms for Liberty Carroll County chapter chair
- Suzie Scott, Moms for Liberty Harford County chapter chair
- Mark Eckstein, co-chair of advocacy for Metro DC PFLAG
- Florence Ashley, assistant professor of law at the University of Alberta

**Caveat**: The Supreme Court [heard arguments in April 2025](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/05/07/supreme-court-hears-maryland-case-on-opting-children-out-from-lgbtq-book-lessons/) on a Montgomery County case about opting out of LGBTQ+ book lessons. This is an active legal case that may have already been decided.

**Story angles**:
- Follow-up on the Supreme Court decision
- Compare policies across all 24 Maryland counties (the data exists from a Maryland Public Information Act request)
- Profile students navigating these policies
- Track school board races and culture war campaign spending

### **Book Bans and Library Censorship**

[Support for book bans varies](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/10/21/support-for-book-bans-varies-in-maryland-school-board-races/) widely among school board candidates. Anne Arundel County took a stand by becoming Maryland's first ["book sanctuary"](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/09/30/officials-set-up-sanctuary-library-fight-book-bans/) following the Freedom to Read Act signed by Gov. Moore.

### **Cellphone Policies**

Maryland schools are [increasingly restricting cellphone use](https://cnsmaryland.org/2024/10/02/phones-off-focus-on-how-maryland-schools-are-cracking-down-on-cell-phones-this-fall/), with some districts piloting locked pouches (like Yondr pouches). Washington County implemented a policy requiring devices off and out of sight during instruction. School board candidates largely support these restrictions, citing cyberbullying and distraction concerns.

## **The Success Stories Worth Following**

Not everything is crisis. Some Maryland schools and districts are finding what works:

### **Restorative Practices**

[Pocomoke High School](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/03/03/in-worcester-county-schools-a-turnaround-based-on-restorative-practices/) in Worcester County adopted restorative practices instead of traditional discipline, leading to a 28% drop in violent incidents and achieving the highest graduation rate in the state. Principal Jenifer Rayne is a source worth cultivating.

### **Graduation Rate Gains**

Maryland's high school [graduation rate reached 88%](https://cnsmaryland.org/2025/04/17/maryland-high-school-graduation-rate-reaches-seven-year-high/) in 2024, a seven-year high, with notable gains among Hispanic students and multilingual learners. Anne Arundel County's "Project Graduation" is worth profiling.

## **Covering This Beat Effectively**

**Attend school board meetings**: They're often online now. Political battles play out here first.

**Read the district implementation plans**: Dry but revealing. What districts say versus what they do tells you where the stories are.

**Follow the money**: Use the "Contracted Out" database and budget documents to understand how districts actually spend funds.

**Build sources at multiple levels**: You need principals, teachers, parents, central office administrators, and state officials. They'll all tell you different things.

**Understand the political context**: Education in Maryland has become increasingly partisan. School board elections matter now in ways they didn't five years ago.

**Remember the kids**: Behind every statistic is a student. Find ways to include student voices, especially from marginalized communities.

**Watch for disparities**: Almost every Maryland education story has a racial equity angle. Black and Hispanic students face higher chronic absenteeism, different discipline rates, and gaps in advanced coursework access.

## **Final Thoughts**

The education beat in Maryland is in a historic moment. The Blueprint represents the most ambitious education reform effort in state history, but it's being implemented during pandemic recovery, a budget crisis, and intense political polarization. The stakes are enormous: how well Maryland educates its 900,000+ public school students will shape the state's economic future and social fabric for decades.

The best stories will connect policy to people—showing how multi-billion-dollar budgets translate into real classrooms, real kids, and real futures. Don't just cover what officials say. Dig into what actually happens when grand plans meet ground-level reality.

Good luck. There are great stories waiting to be told.
