## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

*   **Story 1: Chess for kids, teens at Talbot library**

    *   Event takes place on October 23 at the downtown Easton branch.
    *   Event times are from 4:30 p.m. to 5:30 p.m.
    *   Registration is required at https://talbot.librarycalendar.com/event/afternoon-chess-170

*   **Story 2: Suicide of a superpower**
    *   Max Boot wrote a column in The Washington Post stating that Trump’s cost-cutting measures on universities and research programs are causing “suicide of a superpower.”
    *   Trump is cutting funding for institutions like Princeton University, Columbia University, and the University of Chicago, where scientists like Albert Einstein worked on developing the atomic bomb.
    *   These institutions have contributed to significant technological advancements, including satellite radio, smartphones, and internet development.

*   **Story 3: School lunches through the years**
    *   School lunches have changed over the years but remain important for students’ success.
    *   Marie Savage shares recipes from her childhood in the 1950s, such as bologna sandwiches and homemade soup.
    *   Modern school lunch ideas include BBQ Chicken Cups, PB&J Apples, Pickle Roll Ups, Turkey Club Roll Ups, Grilled Cheese Roll Ups, Nutella Banana Sushi, Ham, Mac N Cheese Muffins, Rice Krispie Peanut Butter Bananas, Pepperoni Pizza Sticks, and other creative options.

*   **Story 4: Student seeks to share Choptank River history**
    *   Kennedy Thomason, a 2019 graduate of Ridgely’s North Caroline High School, worked towards an associate’s degree in liberal arts at Chesapeake College and later transferred to Washington College to major in American studies.
    *   She currently works as a digital archiving intern at the Starr Center for the American Experience.
    *   Thomason plans to revamp her family’s river cruise business, River City Cruise Company, to incorporate more historical tours of the Choptank River.

*   **Story 5: Rocketeers explode onto the scene at nearby farm**
    *   The Maryland Delaware Rocketry Association Inc. launched rockets at Central Sod Farm in Centreville on May 17, 2025, following safety guidelines set by the FCC and Tripoli Rocket Association.
    *   Hobbyists, including members of the Yorktown Aerospace Club and youth from local communities, launched various types of rockets, with some reaching altitudes of over 1,000 feet.
    *   The event was a learning opportunity for students in Science Technology Engineering Mathematics field.

*   **Story 7: Public TV plans shows on Black colleges, universities**
    *   Maryland Public Television will celebrate Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) in September with its fourth annual HBCU Week.
    *   The week-long event will feature over 27 hours of content exploring the past, present, and future of HBCUs from Sept. 4-10.
    *   Highlights include a “Direct Connection” special premiering on-air at 7 p.m. on Sept. 4, and two virtual events: a career opportunity forum and a screening event hosted by Morgan State University student hosts.

*   **Story 8: Bringing history alive: 9th annual GES Wax Museum honors leaders in African American history**
    *   The ninth annual Living Wax Museum was held at Grasonville Elementary School on February 21, 2025, where 75 fourth-graders recreated influential African American figures as living wax figures.
    *   Students studied prominent figures in African American history throughout February, including Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and Serena Williams.

*   **Story 9: Four years after her murder, Madison Sparrow's legacy lives on**
    *   Madison Sparrow was a 17-year-old Newark Charter School student murdered by her ex-boyfriend and friend in Delaware on October 2, 2020.
    *   Four years later, her legacy lives on through five scholarship recipients pursuing careers in education, funded by the Madison Sparrow Scholarship.
    *   Heather Sparrow Murphy, Madison's mother, continues to raise funds for the scholarship and participate in victim-offender dialog programs to help heal.
    *   Murphy forgave her daughter’s murderers before sentencing and has since been involved with the nonprofit Victims' Voices Heard.
    *   The Love Wins 5K race, now in its fourth year, supports the scholarship fund and helps Murphy keep Madison’s memory alive.

*   **Story 10: For All Seasons to host Back to School Resource Fair**
    *   For All Seasons will host its annual School Supplies Giveaway and Resource Fair on August 16 at Idlewild Park in Easton, Maryland.
    *   The event is a partnership with Talbot County Department of Social Services, offering community resources, free snacks, and school supplies.
    *   Last year's event drew over 800 participants.
    *   Vendors include Mid Shore Behavioral Health Inc., University of Maryland Clark Comprehensive Breast Center, and Easton Elementary School.

*   **Story 11: Board of Education Member Donna DiGiacomo resigns**
    *   Donna DiGiacomo resigns from Caroline County Board of Education after three years and four months citing concerns over Superintendent Derek Simmons' use of the term "minions" to describe employees during a March 5 meeting.
    *   DiGiacomo had emailed her resignation to Maryland Superintendent of Education on Monday, March 25, 2024.
    *   Her term was set to expire in 2027.

*   **Story 17: St. Mary's board announces new school cellphone infractions**
    *   St. Mary's public schools' superintendent Scott Smith has instituted a new cellphone policy starting July 17.
    *   The policy restricts phone use to lunch hour for high school students and prohibits middle school and elementary school students from using their devices during the school day.
    *   Consequences include confiscation of phones, parental notification, and disciplinary codes, with repeat offenders facing more severe penalties.
