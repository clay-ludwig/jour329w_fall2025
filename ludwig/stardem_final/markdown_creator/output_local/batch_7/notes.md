## Caroline County Education Beat Book - Notes

**STORY 1: Talbot Board of Ed OKs contracts for EHS auditorium, Chapel Elementary windows**

*   Talbot Board of Education approved contracts for two projects.
*   $1.1 million auditorium lights at Easton High School to be installed in summer 2026.
*   $132,000 window glazing with security film at Chapel District Elementary School in Cordova, Maryland, expected to be completed this summer.

**STORY 2: Elks National chief spotlights community impact in regional meet**

*   The Benevolent and Protective Order of Elks hosted its Maryland, Delaware and District of Columbia regional conference March 21-23 on Kent Island.
*   National leader Douglas A. Schiefer was a keynote speaker with the theme "Elkdom's HEART for America."
*   The local lodge has donated over $100,000 annually to charities, including the National Cancer Society and Boys & Girls Scouting programs.
*   Douglas A. Schiefer is the Grand Exalted Ruler from Bucyrus, Ohio, while Angela Meyer is the current president of the Maryland, Delaware and DC area.

**STORY 3: School board adopts 2024-2025 calendar**

*   The Caroline County Public Schools Board of Education voted unanimously to adopt a new school year calendar for the 2024-2025 school year, starting on Wednesday, August 28.
*   Dr. Derek Simmons presented two alternatives, which were favored by 70% of staff and 75% of parents who responded to a survey.
*   The pre-Labor Day start was adopted.
*   The calendar includes winter break starting December 20 with a half-day Friday and students returning to school on January 7.

**STORY 4: Should Maryland schools ban cellphones?**

*   Maryland's school board candidates largely support stricter cellphone policies in schools.
*   64 of 74 candidates favored restrictions.
*   Some candidates aim to minimize cellphone use during instructional time.
*   Annette Anderson, deputy director of the Johns Hopkins Center for Safe and Healthy Schools, cites concerns over mental health, internet addiction, and social connectivity.
*   Many candidates cite disciplinary issues, cyberbullying, and phone addiction as reasons for restricting cellphone use.

**STORY 5: Chesapeake Forum announces fall preview party**

*   Chesapeake Forum announces its Fall Preview Party from 3:30 to 5:30 p.m. on August 22 at the Academy Art Museum in Easton, MD.
*   The event is free but requires registration.
*   Chesapeake Forum's new speaker series, Lectures on the Shore, launches on October 5 at the museum.
*   Registration for fall classes starts on August 23, and the organization offers 36 courses this term, including a tour of the Delaware Botanical Gardens.

**STORY 6: Rice named new Echo Hill outdoor school executive director**

*   Peter P. Rice III has been appointed as the new executive director of Echo Hill Outdoor School in Worton, Maryland.
*   He was previously an integral part of the school's team since 2014.
*   Rice succeeds his father, Peter Rice Jr., who led the institution for 51 years until his recent retirement.
*   Rice brings a wealth of experience in outdoor education, event management, and strategic planning to the role.

**STORY 7: Local student wins national essay contest**

*   Viana Johnston, an eighth-grade student at Lighthouse Christian Academy in Stevensville, won the national middle school winner of a Bill of Rights Essay contest hosted by the Stanley M. Herzog Foundation in 2023.
*   The contest was part of the foundation's program to advance Christian education.
*   Viana received $250 and her school $250 as prize money.
*   Her essay emphasized the significance of the Bill of Rights, ratified on December 15, 1791.

**STORY 8: Beloved bus driver 'Mrs. Dea' brings holiday cheer to young passengers**

*   Nedean Crawford, known as "Mrs. Dea," is a beloved bus driver in Cecil County, Maryland, who has been driving for 28 years and averages around 61 students per semester.
*   She makes an impact on many lives as a positive female role model, greeting each student entering and leaving the bus daily, and spending extra time getting to know them.
*   Mrs. Dea's route includes Elk Neck Elementary, North East High School, and their Twilight program for students who need extra help.
*   She is known for her holiday cheer, decorating her bus with lights, garland, and singing festive songs with her students.

**STORY 9: Talibah Chikwendu appointed to Dorchester Board of Education**

*   Talibah Chikwendu has been appointed by the Dorchester County Council to fill the vacant seat on the District 2 Dorchester County Board of Education, which ends in 2026.
*   The appointment was made due to Theresa Stafford's passing, who served from 2022 until her death in mid-December.
*   Chikwendu is a graduate of Dorchester County Public Schools and has worked as a school librarian for 10 years and a high school teacher for the past five years.
*   She aims to serve the community and bring value through transparency and acclimating herself to district needs, amidst budget season.

**STORY 10: Making history - Students work towards National History Day competition**

*   Queen Anne's County history teachers Thomas Hayman and Andy Anders are recruiting students for National History Day competition.
*   The Upper Shore region is now second in the state for participation, up from a lack of interest years ago.
*   The 2024 NHD theme is "Turning Points in History," with projects including documentaries, exhibits, performances, and websites that can be done individually or in groups.
*   Research assistance is being provided by public libraries across Queen Anne's, Kent, Talbot, and Dorchester counties.

**STORY 11: DCPS not moving forward with new alternative education programming**

*   Dorchester County Public Schools terminated contracts with alternative education companies in October and November 2023 for elementary and middle school students and high schoolers, respectively.
*   DCPS Superintendent Jymil Thompson recommended not pursuing new contracts due to budgetary restraints.
*   The district will continue staffing its Positive Change Academy on Glasgow Street.

**STORY 12: Talbot Board of Education/July 16**

*   The Talbot County Board of Education approved a new strategic plan on July 16.
*   The board also introduced policy updates on employee code of conduct and video recording in schools.
*   Chief Financial Officer Sarah Jones discussed narrowing down retirement plan options from seven to one, with finalists to be interviewed in August and potentially implemented this October.
*   The district reviewed data from the 2024-2025 school year and heard an update on the construction of a staff health center expected to finish by September.

**STORY 13: Cynthia Marrah Jurris**

*   Cynthia Marrah Jurris, a 65-year-old Easton resident and mediation law expert, is running for the District 6 seat on the Talbot County Board of Education.
*   She is married with three sons who graduated from Talbot County Public Schools.
*   Her priorities include fostering academic excellence, supporting educators, and creating a thriving school environment.
*   The most important issue facing Talbot County Public Schools is advancing academic proficiency, which can be addressed by providing innovative support, engaging families, and using technology effectively.
