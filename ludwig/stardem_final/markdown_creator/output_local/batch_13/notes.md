## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

**Dorchester County Public Schools:**

*   Board of Education Candidates: Prioritizing transparent leadership, engagement, clear communication channels, soliciting feedback, collaborating with community partners, and prioritizing school safety.
*   Goals: Improving academic outcomes, fostering parent and community involvement, addressing chronic absenteeism and discipline issues, and promoting college and career readiness.
*   Blueprint for Maryland's Future: County should fully fund, including increasing teacher pay and partnering with Historically Black Colleges and Universities to support diverse teachers and leaders.
*   Kindergarten Readiness: Increased from 32% to 39% – still needing improvement.
*   Strategies for Improvement: Expanding access to high-quality pre-K, increasing parent and community engagement, providing professional development for early childhood educators, and implementing evidence-based practices.
*   Strategic Plan: Dorchester County Public Schools is developing a strategic plan with Integrated Impact Group for the years 2025-2028. Consultant Connie Casson led the meeting on September 19, outlining the process and goals, including preparing students, recruiting and retaining staff, and exploring career opportunities in local industries. Review data and research districts through September and October, followed by community meetings starting October 14 at Hurlock United Methodist Church.
*   Superintendent David Bromwell stepping down March 1, term ending June 30. Board to vote on interim superintendent at next meeting.
*   Jymil Thompson selected as next Dorchester County Public Schools superintendent, taking over July 1, succeeding Bromwell. Board president praised Thompson as “best fit.”
*   Dr. Sarah Crebs-Oberdorf earned her doctoral degree in contemporary literacy studies from Salisbury University on Nov. 27, 2023. She defended her dissertation titled "An Inquiry Into a Collaborative Teacher Study Group: How do Elementary Teachers of Reading talk about the Intersection Between Race and Reading Instruction and Assessment?" on Jan. 9, 2024, with CCPS Superintendent Dr. Derek Simmons in attendance. Crebs-Oberdorf began her career as a special education teacher in Caroline County districts and served as assistant principal at Ridgely Elementary School.

**Caroline County Public Schools:**

*   Energy Conservation Program with Cenergistic resulted in $260,000 in savings since July 2022. Program focused on reducing energy use and use reduction through measures like solar usage and thermostats. Superintendent Dr. Derek Simmons and Client Manager Scott Muir credited Josh Rein for his work in the district, which also resulted in a 35% decrease in energy use intensity (EUI) from 50 to 32 EUI.
*   1,451 metric tons of CO2 emissions avoided – equivalent to 3.5 million miles driven by an average car. Cenergistic has a five-year contract with the school district.
*   MSDE released 2024 Maryland School Report Card data on December 3. School ratings have increased overall compared to the 2022-23 academic year. 41% of schools earned a four- or five-star rating, while 83% earned three stars or more. Deputy State Superintendent Geoff Sanderson reported that nearly twice as many schools increased their star rating than decreased it. The MSDE is working to improve its accountability system to be more sensitive to granularity growth and develop different benchmark systems for data collection.
*   Caroline Commissioners approved a three-year $1.2 million grant from the Maryland Department of Education to fund summer learning centers for 1,100 students in 2024 and 400 students in 2025-2026. Commission President Travis Breeding discussed reconfiguring the director of Public Works position, citing concerns about its scope and potential need to be contracted out.
*   Twelve Caroline County Public Schools students earned academic honors from the College Board's National Recognition Programs, recognized at the November 7 Board of Education meeting. The recipients include Emmy Brown-Varon, Rebeccah Chambers, Leland Courie, Felix Fimbres, Brooke Gauck, Dallas Helsel, Shelby MacFarland, Leslie Monter Casio, Molly Coughenour, Riley Schall, Eric Shockley, and Bailey Thayer.
*   CTE students at Queen Anne's County High School collaborated with Chesapeake College to build a mock house in the Tech Building from February to March 2025. The project, led by Carpentry Instructor Ron Frederick, taught students carpentry and masonry skills while framing real-world experience. Students gained knowledge in multiple trades, including electricity, plumbing, and HVAC, under the guidance of Queen Anne's County High School career coach Betsy Ricketts.
*   North Dorchester: Hannah Allen and Thomas Allen were killed in a car accident on June 2, 2025, according to a Star Democrat news document. Ty'Ron Anderson and Kaden Andrews are among 67 students listed as survivors of the crash. The victims were from North Dorchester County in Maryland.
*   "ArtScene | the Podcast" launched a new episode on September 1, 2024, featuring Centreville Middle School art teacher Jackie Jablecki. Jablecki shares her journey from personal loss to professional triumph in the classroom, highlighting her passion for art and education. She discusses transformative power of TAB classrooms and student-centered approach. Jablecki is involved with the Art of Education University, where she completed her Master's degree.
*   Morgan Chambers, a 9-year-old student at Calvert Elementary, participated in "Read to the Animals," a program where students read books to retired horses at Freedom Hill Horse Rescue in Owings, MD, on August 12, 2025. The program is part of a partnership between Calvert public schools and Freedom Hill, with an aim to improve reading fluency and animal comfort while promoting service and compassion. Founded 21 years ago, Freedom Hill Horse Rescue welcomes students from kindergarten to fifth-grade for the "Read to the Animals" program, which has been well-received by both children and horses. The initiative is expected to inspire some participants to consider volunteering at the rescue center when older.
*   Cenergistic implemented a yearlong energy conservation program with Cenergistic in July 2022, resulting in $260,000 in savings since then. The program focused on reducing energy use and use reduction through measures like solar usage and thermostats. Superintendent Dr. Derek Simmons and Client Manager Scott Muir credited Josh Rein for his work in the district, which also resulted in a 35% decrease in energy use intensity (EUI) from 50 to 32 EUI.
