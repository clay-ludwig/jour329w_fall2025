## Caroline County Education - Beat Book Notes (December 14, 2025)

**STORY 1: 'Meeting of the hearts': Women to Watch panelists share advice on mindfulness**

*   The Mid-Shore Women to Watch speaker series hosted women at The Market at Dover Station.
*   Panelists included Anne Altvater, Morgan Foster, Shania Gregory, and Amber McGinnis.
*   Panelists shared tips on mindfulness, including letting emotions be present and avoiding technology while stopped at a red light.
*   APG Chesapeake hosted the event, moderated by Cassandra Vanhooser.
*   A second panel is scheduled for August 14 at White Oak Manor in Cecil County.
*   This year’s panelists will select their successors for the 2026 Summer Speaker Series.

**STORY 2: QACPS approves piggybacking bread and paper product contracts**

*   Queen Anne’s County Public Schools (QACPS) approved piggybacking contracts.
*   Schmidt Baking Company was awarded the bread product contract.
*   FPC Distribution was awarded the cafeteria paper product contract.
*   The fiscal impact for bread products is expected to be $140,000.
*   The fiscal impact for paper products is expected to be $142,000.
*   Both contracts are effective June 5, 2024.
*   The contracts are from the food service fund balance.

**STORY 4: CarePacks awarded $15K from 100+ Women Who Care Talbot County**

*   CarePacks of Talbot County received $15,000 in funding from 100+ Women Who Care Talbot County.
*   Emily Moody and Easton Mayor Megan Cook founded 100+ Women Who Care Talbot County.
*   The program aims to provide nutrition support to Talbot County students who rely on school meals over weekends.
*   The group has seen steady growth in membership since its inaugural meeting in September.
*   Members donate $300 per year, and the group meets quarterly to discuss funding for local nonprofits.

**STORY 5: KIHS' Mamas named among finalists for Maryland Teacher of the Year**

*   Marcie Mamas, a business teacher at Kent Island High School in Stevensville, Maryland, is one of seven finalists for the 2026 Maryland Teacher of the Year award.
*   Mamas has taught for 30 years.
*   Mamas previously won the Maryland Business Teacher of the Year award.
*   The winner will be announced on September 26, 2025, and compete for the National Teacher of the Year Award in April 2026.

**STORY 6: Girls' STEM gains are slipping**

*   Girls' STEM gains are declining at a faster rate than boys since the pandemic.
*   Mid-Shore school districts show this trend.
*   A statewide group focused on youth STEM programming was contacted for answers, but district representatives were unresponsive to initial inquiries.

**STORY 7: Talbot County wrestles with schools funding request, long-term Blueprint concerns**

*   Talbot County Council voted not to include an additional $5.7 million in funding for Talbot County Public Schools in the fiscal year 2026 budget.
*   The county’s draft budget already includes around $55 million in required education funding as mandated by The Blueprint for Maryland’s Future.
*   Superintendant Sharon Pepukayi said the district will have to prioritize and make tough decisions if the county only provides the minimum required funding.
*   A joint meeting found common ground on long-term sustainability of the state’s Blueprint initiative, with officials agreeing it will take time and strategic planning to meet the increasing funding requirements set by 2034.

**STORY 8: Author empowering young voices**

*   Jessieca Savage reflects on her school days in Bishopville and shares memories of school lunches in the 1950s to 2025.
*   Schools have changed their lunch options over time, with popular items including spaghetti, submarines, and a la carte foods.
*   In recent years, there has been an increase in allergens such as peanuts, gluten, and dairy, leading to more limited menu options.
*   Savage shares recipes for convenient and affordable packed lunches, including BBQ chicken cups, PB&J apples, and grilled cheese roll-ups.

**STORY 10: Education workers empty their offices after recent layoffs**

*   Education workers in the US Department of Education packed up their desks after layoffs aimed at reducing the workforce by 50% due to a "reduction in force" process that bypassed seniority and performance-based rankings.
*   Approximately 2,200 employees received early buyouts or retirements.
*   Around 1,300 employees were subject to layoff notices.
*   Former employees gathered outside the department's headquarters in solidarity, protesting what they called an "assault on public education."
*   Demonstrations grew from a small gathering at the Office of the State Superintendent of Education to a larger event at the Department of Education's headquarters.

**STORY 11: ‘Stretched very thin’: Child care providers in Talbot County seeing long waitlists**

*   Critchlow Adkins Children’s Centers in Talbot County has over 100 children on its waitlist for preschool and before- and after-school care services.
*   The nonprofit plans to expand its facility at Chapel District Elementary School, doubling capacity from 30 to 60 children, by next year.
*   Barbra Diaz, director of the Talbot County Center for Children and Families, notes that staffing is the main challenge in the program’s Early Head Start initiative.
*   The Lab Child Care Center in Easton also has a rolling waitlist for under-2-year-olds due to rising costs and state scholarship program issues.

**STORY 12: ‘Open up worlds’: Donor gifts $1 million to BAAM girls program**

*   Dr. Eileen Amy Ryan donated $1 million to Building African American Minds (BAAM) on April 25, 2025.
*   The gift will expand BAAM’s girls program at the middle-school level.
*   BAAM is a nonprofit organization based in Easton, Maryland, led by Executive Director Dina Daly.

**STORY 13: Dorchester Board of Education Policy Committee/Sept.**

*   The Dorchester Board of Education Policy Committee met on September 25 and discussed the following policies:
    *   Policy 470.00 replaced "pupil" with "student".
    *   Policy 470.01 added driver responsibility for suspending students' bus privileges.
    *   Policy 470.02 discussed reinforcing parent contact in one-day suspensions.
    *   Policy 470.03 remained unchanged.
    *   Policy 503.05 proposed reducing max carry to 50 compensation hours within 90 days.
    *   Policy 470.04 remained unchanged.
