## Caroline County Education Beat Book - Notes

**STORY 1: Responsible Fathers Initiative empowers, strengthens men**

*   The Responsible Fathers Initiative, founded by Corey Pack in 2019, empowers men to become better fathers.
*   Uses curriculum developed by the National Fatherhood Initiative.
*   Three in-person groups inside the Talbot County Detention Center and several community-based groups in Easton and Cambridge.
*   Pack’s inspiration came from seeing the negative impact of absent or irresponsible fathers on families, particularly children born into households without a responsible father figure.
*   The initiative has partnered with organizations such as the Neighborhood Service Center to provide wraparound services, including housing, transportation, food, and financial literacy assistance.

**STORY 2: Class of 2024 NDHS eagles fly the nest**

*   North Dorchester High School's Class of 2024 graduated on May 29, with Ta’Tyana Maradiaga and Fatima Kaleem delivering their speeches.
*   The ceremony was held indoors due to rain.
*   Ta’Tyana Maradiaga will attend the University of Maryland, College Park.
*   Fatima Kaleem plans to attend New York University.
*   Principal David Stofa emphasized the importance of embracing diversity, empathy, and kindness as graduates pursue their dreams.
*   Caps flew through the auditorium stage during the graduation ceremony.

**STORY 3: Will Maryland’s Blueprint for education work?**

*   Maryland plans to spend $16.6 billion by 2029 on its Blueprint for education.
*   The Accountability and Implementation Board oversees funding and initiative implementation, monitoring progress through 92 data measures.
*   School districts face challenges due to short planning time, limited resources, and slow communication with employees.
*   The board can withhold funding from failing school systems.
*   The Blueprint's success will be evaluated by October 2025.
*   A deep and lasting crisis remains despite progress.

**STORY 4: Counties sue social media over mental health in schools - Talbot among 10 counties claiming classroom distraction**

*   Charles County Public Schools joined nine other school districts in a lawsuit against Google, Meta, ByteDance and Snap Inc., alleging that social media giants are targeting and manipulating youth to stay engaged for excessive amounts of time.
*   The lawsuit aims to pressure companies to create products geared towards children and address the negative impacts on mental health, including increased self-control issues, emotional regulation problems, and increased aggression among students.

**STORY 5: Removal of Safe Space stickers draws criticism**

*   Melissa Goshorn requested that Superintendent Andraé Townsel remove "Safe Space" stickers from school buildings, citing concerns raised at a National School Boards Association conference.
*   The stickers were removed and drew criticism, with 19 of 20 speakers opposing their removal during a May 8 meeting.
*   Calvert County School Board President Jana Post defended the sticker’s removal as not violating First Amendment rights.
*   A motion to discuss a flag policy was unanimously passed, with Marc Watson appointed as supervisor of health, physical education, and athletics.

**STORY 6: Moms For Liberty fields candidates in elections**

*   Moms For Liberty backs candidates Ann C. O’Connor, Karla Wieland-Cherry, and Kerry Gunshenan in Talbot County Board of Education elections.
*   The group has been accused of promoting anti-LGBTQ+ misinformation and fighting to scrub diverse material from lesson plans.
*   Three candidates submitted questionnaires with no answers, sparking concern among voters.
*   The Talbot County election will give candidates another opportunity to share their stances on issues.
*   Caroline County recently celebrated the business community’s economic development efforts and addressed rising sea levels along the Gulf coast.

**STORY 7: Maryland State of Reading Report: ‘time for a comeback after a decade of decline’**

*   Maryland’s reading achievement has shown incremental gains, but a new report reveals that 67% of fourth-grade students and nearly two-thirds of eighth-grade students cannot read at a proficient level.
*   The Maryland READS "State of Reading in Maryland 2025" report calls for a comprehensive approach to literacy, prioritizing teacher training, community-driven efforts, and addressing excessive screen time.
*   Trish Brennan-Gac, executive director of Maryland READS, criticized recent legislation for missing the mark on funding for literacy coaches.
*   State leaders have taken steps toward a solution, but more work is needed to address the state’s reading crisis.

**STORY 8: It’s time to pause state’s education blueprint**

*   The Blueprint for Maryland’s Future (Kirwan) faces challenges in achieving its goals, including expanding pre-kindergarten participation and funding for schools with high poverty concentrations.
*   Maryland lags behind 17 other states in pre-kindergarten enrollment.
*   The Board of Education faces challenges including significant turnover in its accounting function and requests for increased funding despite flat student enrollment numbers.

**STORY 9: Saints Peter and Paul seniors become alumni**

*   Sts. Peter and Paul High School's Class of 2025 graduates became alumni by shaking hands with Bishop William Koenig at a ceremony on May 28, 2025, in Easton, Maryland.
*   Associate Pastor Michael Angeloni led prayers, while Senior Class President Francesca Villano shared memories of her classmates' experiences.

**STORY 10: Wright named North Caroline assistant principal**

*   Dr. Derek Simmons announced Chastity Wright's appointment as assistant principal of North Caroline High School at the February 6 Board of Education meeting.
*   Wright has been dean of students since the start of the school year and was chosen unanimously by an interview committee in 2012.

**STORY 11: Three candidates compete for District 5 Dorchester Board of Education seat**

*   Chris Wheedleton, Mike Hartford, and Jerome Harris are running for the District 5 Dorchester County Board of Education seat.
*   Chris Wheedleton has been serving as a board member and focuses on addressing the need for clear direction and accountability in the school district.
*   Mike Hartford aims to bring transparency and involve parents in decision-making processes.
*   Kindergarten readiness among Dorchester County kindergarteners is at 39% this school year compared to 32% the previous year, according to DCPS data.

**STORY 12: Art teachers recognized by Retired School Personnel Association**

**STORY 13: County agrees to audit of school system financial records**

*   The Board of Education faces challenges including significant turnover in its accounting function and requests for increased funding despite flat student enrollment numbers.

**STORY 14: Open house, open doors, open minds and hearts**

*   Open Doors Partners in Education, a non-profit organization, was founded in fall 2020 and has provided tutoring and literacy support to over 100 individuals in Kent County and surrounding communities since its opening.
*   Executive Director Ashley Singer hosted an open house event at their center on February 1, 2025, which attracted over 50 attendees including board members, staff, students, and donors.
*   The organization utilizes the Orton-Gillingham approach, a multi-sensory method to help struggling readers.
*   As of 2022, they had enrolled 57 students with instruction provided at no cost or low cost.
