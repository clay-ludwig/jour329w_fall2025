## Caroline County Education Beat Book - Notes (December 14, 2025)

**STORY 1: 'Love In Action' displayed by Black Excellence fund**

*   The Black Excellence Community Fund (BECF) awarded $500 grants to three Lower Shore nonprofits during an August 5 evening dinner event celebrating Black Philanthropy Month in Salisbury, MD.
*   The grantees were Y.E.E.S. W.E. C.A.N., SchooLARS 4 STEM, and Faith Over Fear Economic Empowerment Inc.
*   BECF was created in 2022 to promote cultural awareness and pride through education, health, and welfare benefits for the Black community.

**STORY 2: No one knows how much Maryland’s education reform will cost**

*   Maryland’s Democratic-led legislature passed the Blueprint for Maryland’s Future in 2021, aiming to pour billions into public schools, but didn’t outline long-term funding plans.
*   The General Assembly will start running a structural deficit by fiscal year 2025, growing nearly sixfold by fiscal year 2029, and estimating over $4 billion in costs by then.
*   State officials have offered various solutions, including raising taxes or cutting back on the Blueprint’s demands, but no consensus has been reached.
*   Governor Wes Moore and Democrats support the current plan, while Republicans advocate for a "Blueprint lite" approach.
*   The Accountability and Implementation Board is expected to issue recommendations regarding the budget challenges.
*   Key figures include: Hezekiah Herrera, special education teacher; Stefano Lodola, owner of Think Languages LLC; Ben Wallerstein, CEO of Whiteboard Advisors; Patrick Riccards, former education advisor; Linda McMahon, Trump’s pick for education secretary.
*   Dates: 2024 campaign and election; 1975 (approval of IDEA)
*   Places: United States, San Diego, California, Washington D.C., Falls Church, Virginia, Fort Lauderdale, Florida

**STORY 3: Talbot council mulling $1.1 million request from school district**

*   Talbot County Council considers a $1.1 million request from Talbot County Public Schools for fiscal year 2026, including $600,000 for new positions and $500,000 for teacher salary increases.
*   The district’s previous request was $5.7 million.
*   County leaders are weighing the best option as they prepare to vote on final amendments to the county’s draft $161 million budget on May 27.
*   Talbot County is already mandated by the state to pay $440,000 for teachers’ pensions and has considered three potential tax supplement scenarios.

**STORY 4: New student board member sworn-in**

*   Alden Swanson, a rising junior at Kent County High School, was sworn-in as the newest member of the Kent County Board of Education on August 12.
*   She will serve a one-year term and provide a monthly report, cast an advisory vote, and offer student perspective on issues.
*   Swanson was elected by her peers at Kent County High School and is a member of the cross country team and band.

**STORY 5: Women to Watch event recognizes more than 200 local leaders**

*   More than 200 women from the Mid-Shore region were nominated for the annual Women to Watch magazine, with 16 selected by the committee to be featured in this year’s issue.
*   The event was held at The Avalon Theatre in Easton on April 14, 2024, and honored local leaders such as Ivette Furneisen (lawyer), Sharon Pepukayi (superintendent of Talbot County Public Schools), and Meisha Amia (nurse, author, and entrepreneur).
*   The Women to Watch magazine aims to highlight impactful work being done by women in the Mid-Shore community.

**STORY 6: Safe havens of knowledge**

*   Thomas Jefferson believed that an informed citizenry was essential to a democracy’s success.
*   The American Library Association released preliminary data documenting 414 attempts to censor library materials and services in public, school, and academic libraries during the first eight months of 2024, with 47% targeting books representing people of color and LGBTQ+ voices.
*   Banned Books Week is being commemorated on October 19, Freedom to Read Day of Action, by Unite Against Book Bans.

**STORY 7: Carefully vet candidates**

*   Moms For Liberty co-founder Bridget Zielgler was involved in an investigation, and the organization’s mission statement is to defend parents' rights at all levels of government.
*   The group has members running for positions affecting school children in Talbot County, which raises concerns about infiltrating the school system with their doctrine.
*   The MFL manifesto does not mention the rights of children.
*   Sharon Harrington plans to carefully vet candidates who are part of the organization.

**STORY 8: Talbot school board discusses concerns over athletic fields**

*   Talbot County Board of Education discussed athletic fields on September 20, 2024, with concerns over costs and maintenance.
*   The district has $1.5 million for capital projects, including an estimated $1.8 million for an artificial turf field at Easton High School.
*   Other projects include auditorium lights, a basketball court, stadium lights, and a new playground, totaling $3.36 million.
*   Board members expressed concerns about filling vacant positions but ultimately approved the request unanimously on June 27, 2024.

**STORY 9: Foundation of HOPE leading girls to success**

*   Keasha Haythe founded the Foundation of HOPE in Easton, MD, an after-school program for middle school girls on the Eastern Shore that began eight years ago.
*   The program aims to provide cultural, social, spiritual and educational development to help young girls lead productive lives in their communities.
*   John Englehart, assistant manager of human resources for Easton Utilities, led a presentation to the cohort of girls on marketing themselves and achieving career goals.
*   The program covers topics such as low self-esteem, bullying, financial literacy, and workforce development.

**STORY 10: County approves budget transfers for Talbot schools**

*   Talbot County Public Schools requested approval from the county council to make categorical transfers in its FY24 budget, including $75,000 for student services and $500,000 for transportation.
*   The district also sought $600,000 for fixed charges related to other postemployment benefits contributions and health insurance prepayments.
*   Savings from instructional salaries ($850,000) were used to transfer funds.
*   Council members expressed concerns about filling vacant positions but ultimately approved the request unanimously on June 27, 2024.

**STORY 11: Caroline County Public Schools welcomes 40 new teachers**

*   Caroline County Public Schools welcomed 40 new teachers on August 15, 2025, at a breakfast and orientation event.
*   New assistant principals and a supervisor of instruction were also introduced.
*   Superintendent Derek Simmons emphasized the district's motto "One Family — One Vision."
*   Michele Wayman described CCPS as a "small school system doing big things."

**STORY 12: Girl Scout project receives two paws up**

*   Girl Scout Troop 10069 junior members Sarah Mack, Kait Larson, Greta Isom, and Stella Hageman received a Bronze Award for their project "Pet Friendly Patios in Our Community."
*   The project involved creating maps and decals indicating pet-friendly patios at Leonardtown eateries and cafes.
*   The map is available online and on social media, while decals adorn the front doors of four local businesses.
*   The project took around three months to complete and cost approximately $125.
