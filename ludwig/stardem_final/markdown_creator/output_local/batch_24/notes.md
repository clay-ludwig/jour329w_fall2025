## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

*   **BAAM Ribbon Cutting:** Building African American Minds (BAAM) Inc. announced a ribbon-cutting ceremony for its new academic center on December 14, 2024, at 10 a.m. The event will be held at BAAM Athletic Center in Easton, MD, featuring remarks from organizational leaders and community partners. Dina Gomes Daly, executive director of BAAM, described the center as a "space where learning, growth and community come alive."

*   **Scholar to Discuss Anna Douglass:** Dr. Celeste-Marie Bernier will discuss Anna Murray Douglass at the Museum of Rural Life in Denton, MD on November 29, from 5 to 6:30 p.m. The event is co-sponsored by the Caroline County Public Library and the Frederick Douglass Honor Society.

*   **Schools to Remain Closed Friday:** Talbot County Public Schools and Caroline County Public Schools announced closure on January 10 due to inclement weather, the fifth day of closure, which may impact spring semester academic calendar. Dorchester County Public Schools students will have a virtual instruction day instead.

*   **Maryland Schools Cracking Down on Cell Phones:** Maryland schools are cracking down on cell phones this fall, with at least a third of public schools tightening up rules to keep devices off and out of sight. The new rules apply to instruction periods, even on school buses in some areas around Baltimore. Two middle schools in Caroline County have implemented a pilot program requiring students to lock their phones in pouches during the day. Supt. Derek L. Simmons in Caroline County and Supt. Scott Smith in St. Mary’s County are among administrators promoting the measures.

*   **Washington College Receives $4.5 Million Gift:** Richard and Tara Wood have made a $4.5 million gift to Washington College in Chestertown, Maryland. The gift includes funding for endowed chairs in leadership ($2.5 million) and international business ($750,000), as well as an indoor tennis facility ($1 million) and the sailing program ($250,000).

*   **“Contracted Out” Database:** The University of Maryland's Philip Merrill College of Journalism published an easily searchable database, “Contracted Out,” which provides a comprehensive and unprecedented itemization of school contract spending statewide in Maryland over the past four years. The database contains information from all 24 public school districts in Maryland, including vendor payments for health insurance, buses, computers, and meals, with data from 2018-2019 to 2021-2022 school years. The database was compiled by students and faculty at the UMD Philip Merrill College of Journalism over seven months, with assistance from the state’s Office of Legislative Audits and other organizations. The project aims to increase transparency and accountability in school district spending.

*   **Washington College Electric Boat Team Visit:** The Washington College Electric Boat Team visited Radcliffe Creek School on September 20, with President Douglas Hewes and advisor Brian Palmer sharing their team's success story. The team won first place in multiple races against top engineering programs, overcoming design failures to achieve success. They broke the Guinness World Record for the greatest distance by an electric boat on a single charge without solar on September 2.

*   **Caroline County Public Library Renovation:** Caroline County Public Library celebrated the completion of a years-long renovation on May 22, 2024. The renovated space offers various programming for patrons of different ages, including gaming stations and study rooms. Connie Kumor, an architect, led the design process, aiming to create a more friendly and open environment. Executive Director Debbie Bennett, retiring at the end of June, cited major legacies, including the Maryland State Library Capital Grant and community donations. The library was supported by state legislators and the Friends of the Library, which raised $78,000 for furniture.

*   **DECEMBER at Chesapeake College:** Chesapeake College will host “THE SNOW SHOW” holiday musical production at TPAC on December 8-9 and 12-14. Info sessions for truck driving careers, skilled trades programs, and winter break schedules are scheduled. The college is closed from December 20-24 and classes begin January 8-9 for entry-level marine service technician and V6 engine service students.

*   **Students Experience Farm to School Eating:** Teachers and volunteers led students to a field outside Mechanicsville Elementary School, where they experienced Farm to School events. Kaitlin Fraer organized events at three elementary schools in St. Mary’s County for National Farm to School Month. The events included stations about farm animals, fruits, vegetables, and healthy eating, with guest speakers Beth Palumbo and Christine Allred. Students also planted sweet potatoes and met farm animals, including a blind horse named Willa.

*   **QA Advocates for Youth appoints new director:** Queen Anne’s County Advocates for Youth Inc. appointed Judy Corchiarino as executive director and elected three new board members: Lynn Engelmeyer, Carol Franks-Randall, and Erin Coyne. Corchiarino, a retired food service executive, will oversee day-to-day operations and serve as liaison with member agencies. The new board members are: Engelmeyer, a native Marylander; Franks-Randall, a retired educator; and Coyne, an elementary school teacher.

*   **Dorchester BOE passes emergency virtual education plan with amendment:** The Dorchester County Board of Education passed an emergency virtual education plan with amendments on May 22, 2024. The original plan, required by Maryland Senate bill, includes information on staffing and student instruction during prolonged emergencies. The board voted to amend the plan to make content supervisor involvement optional rather than mandatory in Schoology classrooms. The board also discussed camera usage in virtual classrooms, with concerns about bandwidth issues, and considered adding a provision for cameras to be turned off in such cases.

*   **Chrome City fundraiser revs up Benedictine's Ridgely campus:** Benedictine School held its 22nd annual Chrome City Fundraiser on July 27, 2024, at its Ridgely campus. The event drew hundreds of car and motorcycle enthusiasts, with proceeds supporting the school’s mission to care for children with special needs.

*   **Tcps Gives Update On Its Priorities At Board Meeting:** Talbot County Public Schools' Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi reported on the school system’s priorities, which include academic equity and achievement, fostering an engaging environment, and hiring and retaining highly qualified and diverse staff. Academic equity and achievement strategies included implementing additional interventions and assessments, while fostering an engaging environment involved school-based mental health services and restorative practices training. The district has seen improvement in student and teacher attitudes towards safety and substance abuse since conducting climate surveys in 2022. Since the start of the year, TCPS has hired 90 new staff members, with a focus on retaining existing employees to address national education shortages.

*   **Cell phone pouches coming to St. Michaels Middle next year:** The Talbot County Board of Education voted unanimously to introduce cell phone pouches at St. Michaels Middle School for the 2025-2026 school year. A similar program has been piloted at Easton Middle School since December, with improved student engagement and a decrease in bullying reported. The board will not implement pouches at high schools next year but may consider it in the future due to differing opinions on its effectiveness. Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi estimated a cost of $9,000 for the program, while Board member Karla Wieland-Cherry suggested it could cost around $38,000 if expanded to high schools.

*   **Thousands of Maryland students are chronically absent each year:** Here is a summary of the article in 2-5 sentences: Maryland students face high rates of chronic absenteeism, with nearly 27% of students missing 10% or more of school in the 2023-2024 year, an increase from 2018. Baltimore City has the highest chronic absenteeism rate among Maryland jurisdictions, with nearly half of all public school students chronically absent last year. Effective strategies to combat absenteeism include attendance contracts, parent conferences, and incentives like gift cards or snow tubing trips. State education leaders aim to reduce chronic absenteeism rates to 15% by next school year through initiatives such as creating a chronic absenteeism task force and developing a toolkit to address student absenteeism.
