## Caroline County Education Notes - Easton Star-Democrat Beat Book

**Story 1: Rescinded federal reimbursements cause $1.4 million impact on TCPS health center**

*   The TCPS health center in Easton, Maryland has been delayed due to rescinded federal reimbursements from the U.S. Department of Education.
*   Secretary of Education Linda McMahon announced on March 28 that the department would not honor extension requests for pandemic-era funds.
*   Over $400 million of Maryland schools’ funding at risk.
*   Remaining costs for the health center total around $1.4 million.
*   TCPS had paid $127,000 to Marathon Health so far.

**Story 2: Caroline County high schools mark graduations**

*   Two Caroline County high schools celebrated graduations: Colonel Richardson High School on June 5 and North Caroline High School on May 29.
*   Graduates wore their school colors and mortar boards as they walked to the strains of "Pomp and Circumstance."
*   Principals Jared Sherman (CRHS) and Matt Spiker (NCHS) congratulated the graduates.
*   Sherman praised the efforts of many people involved in the graduation.

**Story 3: Chesapeake College honors Class of 2025**

*   Chesapeake College honored its Class of 2025 with a commencement ceremony on May 21, 2025.
*   President Clifford Coppersmith welcomed graduates to new horizons and emphasized hard work and connections.
*   Former U.S. Sen. Ben Cardin received the Community Service Award for his advocacy and dedication to education.
*   Cherie Patchett was awarded the Adjunct Innovative Teaching Award for her innovative teaching methods.
*   Cianni A. Phillips received the John T. Harrison Award for her leadership and empathy.

**Story 4: Talbot school board sees test scores, improvement initiatives**

*   Talbot County Public Schools saw slight increases in math and English proficiency levels.
*   47.3% of students proficient in English and 18.3% in math as of the 2023-2024 Maryland Comprehensive Assessment Program.
*   District led by Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi and Assistant Superintendent Helga Einhorn.
*   Talbot County ranks 14th and 18th among 24 districts in English and math proficiency, respectively.
*   Experts will review three elementary schools in Talbot County, including White Marsh Elementary, which showed significant growth in math achievement.

**Story 5: A year of community outreach and literacy**

*   The Queen Anne’s County Library is celebrating its first-anniversary of the Mobile Library initiative.
*   Event took place on November 1 at Crumpton Park.
*   Tamar Sarnoff, representing the Maryland State Library, was a guest speaker.
*   Director Janet Salazar expressed gratitude to the community for their support and highlighted the library’s dedication to fostering a lifelong love of reading and learning.

**Story 6: St. Mary’s College of Maryland freezes tuition again; President announces retirement**

*   St. Mary’s College of Maryland froze tuition for the fifth consecutive year.
*   President Tuajuanda Jordan announced her retirement in June 2025.
*   College approved a 3.9% increase in room and board rates for the 2024-25 school year due to inflation and the increased Maryland minimum wage.
*   Outgoing Chair Susan L. Dyer led the final meeting, which also saw the election of John Bell as the new chair and approval of various board meetings and events.

**Story 7: Budget discussions kicked off at Talbot Board of Ed work session**

*   Talbot Board of Education discussed preliminary budget considerations at a work session on December 18, 2024, as planning for the fiscal year 2026 budget is underway.
*   Chief Financial Officer Sarah Jones outlined priorities including academic equity, student engagement, and staff recruitment and retention.
*   Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi mentioned special education as a recent focus area.
*   District needs to make shifts in two programs to comply with Blueprint requirements, which affect funding for smaller schools.

**Story 8: CAROLINE COUNTY BOARD OF EDUCATION/ Oct. 7, 2025**

*   The Caroline County Board of Education approved the FY26 Comprehensive Maintenance Plan on Oct. 7, 2025.
*   The plan aims to provide an effective learning environment and minimize disruptions while preserving school property value and maximizing facility efficiency.
*   Director Kirk Howie presented an unofficial September enrollment update showing a total of 5608 students, with decreases in elementary and high schools but increases in home school/instruction enrollments.
*   The board also approved Policy 9910 on Operation School Nutrition Programs and Policy 55080 on service animals, and nominated parents and community members for the Citizens' Advisory Committee for 2025-2027.

**Story 9: Talbot Board of Ed digs into student discipline trends**

*   Talbot County Public Schools officials reviewed district data on suspensions and expulsions during a Board of Education work session.
*   Administrators must indicate an "imminent threat" for expulsion, which has not been the case in the last three full school years.
*   District reported no expulsions or significant out-of-school suspensions so far this year.
*   Talbot County’s suspension and expulsion rates are lower than neighboring counties, with 5% of students suspended or expelled in the 2023-2024 school year.

**Story 10: Amid budget woes, Dorchester cancels summer school, appeals to students to 'get the work done'**

*   Dorchester County Public Schools will not offer summer school in 2025.
*   District led by Interim Superintendent Jymil Thompson.
*   Students were bullied at school.

**Story 17: Community BBQ hosted to show support for DCPS middle schooler**

*   A community BBQ was hosted in Cambridge, MD, on May 8, 2024, to support sixth grader Tori Gibbs, who is Muslim and was bullied at school.
*   Event organized by mosques in Baltimore and Philadelphia.
*   Tori's mother, LaToya Gibbs, met with Interim Superintendent Jymil Thompson on May 8 to discuss bullying awareness.
*   Rian Johnson, a parent involved in the incident, has been charged with conspiracy-related crimes.
*   Five attendees took their Shahada and expressed support for Tori.

**Story 18: Talbot Board of Ed voices mixed opinions over 2025-26 calendar draft**

*   Talbot County Board of Education members voiced mixed opinions on a draft calendar for the 2025-26 school year.
*   Draft included two days off in March and a five-day spring break from April 2 to April 6, 2026.
*   Helga Einhorn presented the draft, which sparked confusion among board members, including Emily Jackson, who called it "surprisingly contentious."
*   Board President Emily Jackson expressed support for extending spring break, while Deborah Bridges liked the two days off in March.
