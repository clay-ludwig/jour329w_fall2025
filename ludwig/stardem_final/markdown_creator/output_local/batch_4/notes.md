## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

*   **Holiday de-stress - Celebration alleviates overstimulation associated with holidays:** The Sensory Holiday Celebration was held at Kent County Middle School on December 27 in its sixth year, organized by the Kent County Infants and Toddlers program. Professionals from various organizations attended, including staff from Judy Centers in Kent County and the Family Resource Center.

*   **MCAP results show signs of recovery from pandemic learning loss - Science scores still show decline:** Maryland Comprehensive Assessment Program (MCAP) test results show that student proficiency in English Language Arts has surpassed pre-pandemic levels, with an average passing rate of 47% in 2023. Math scores have improved but still lag behind pre-pandemic levels, with a 24% average passing rate in 2023. Science scores, particularly for grade 8, continue to decline, with a historic low in 2023.

*   **Mid-Shore program aims to teach kids about health care careers:** Three Mid-Shore women - Dr. Judith Gieske, Ruth Ann Jones, and Cynthia Watson - are launching "Introduction to Careers in Healthcare" (ITCH), a program to introduce the next generation to the healthcare industry on the Eastern Shore.

*   **Midshore Technology Services to work with student apprentice:** Kyren Hawkins signed a youth apprenticeship contract with Justin Smith, president of Midshore Technology Services in Denton, on November 12, 2024. Hawkins is currently enrolled in cybersecurity at North Caroline High School. This is Midshore Technology Services' second youth apprenticeship agreement, with the first occurring in 2021 with Christopher Ireland.

*   **Master Gardeners keep rooted in the Kent:** Kent County Commissioners awarded approximately $20,000 to keep the Master Gardeners program running, including funding for a part-time Master Gardener Coordinator and Horticulture Educator. The program, established in 2007, provides free educational programming and services valued at $29,369 in 2023. Kent County Master Gardeners had visited commissioners due to concerns about disbanding the program. President Ron Fithian, Albert Nickerson, and John Price unanimously voted in favor of the funding request.

*   **Dorchester County Council makes appointments to vacant Board of Education seats:** Dorchester County Council appointed Brandy Dawson Cumberland and Christine LaMonica to fill Board of Education vacancies created by Mike Diaz's and Susan Morgan's resignations. Cumberland was appointed to District 1, while LaMonica was appointed to District 3. The appointments took effect immediately. Cumberland and LaMonica will prioritize a "safe learning environment," school safety, transparency, academic excellence, and support for educators.

*   **Dorchester MCAP data shows some progress, some areas of concern:** Dorchester County Public Schools' Spring 2024 MCAP scores show year-to-year increases in English and math proficiency rates. The district ranked 22nd in English and 21st in math, with notable improvements in fifth grade science and middle school algebra. Concerns remain about support pathway classes, elementary English scores, and literacy plan implementation. Superintendent Jymil Thompson noted the score increases as progress, while Assistant Superintendent Jodi Colman highlighted growth areas and called for further improvement.

*   **Howie sworn into vacant Board of Education seat:** Anna Howie was sworn in as the newest member of the Talbot County Board of Education on October 16, 2023, by Governor Wes Moore, following Susan Delean-Botkin's resignation. Howie will serve until early December 2024. She is a curriculum supervisor for Dorchester County Public Schools and has 21 years of experience in K-12 education. The Talbot County Education Association endorsed her for the appointment.
