## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

**STORY 1: If we want reading success, we need to invest**

*   Maryland is set to release its 2025 MCAP test scores, likely to reflect years of missed investments in reading education since 2013.
*   State Superintendent Carey Wright has established frameworks and infrastructure for real change, including a pre-K–3 Literacy Policy and a statewide coaching model.
*   Wright has secured nearly $50 million in federal and private funds, but these investments are time-limited and unsustainable without a permanent state commitment.
*   To achieve dramatic improvement, Maryland needs to invest more than just hiring the right superintendent, requiring sustained resources from the legislature.

**STORY 2: Mother can't provide holiday celebration while in school**

*   A mother of three is in school to obtain her nursing certification and struggles to provide a holiday celebration for her children due to living expenses and school costs.
*   The Brighter Christmas Fund, a nonprofit helping families in need on the Mid-Shore, will assist with her family's holiday celebration.
*   Donations can be sent to The Star Democrat at 29088 Airpark Dr., Easton, MD 21601 or made online via credit card or PayPal.
*   As of November 15, 2024, the total donated for this year's Brighter Christmas Fund is $27,643.75.

**STORY 3: St. Michaels library looks to expand its footprint**

*   The Talbot County Free Library St. Michaels Branch plans a 3,700-square-foot expansion to be completed by December 2025 or January 2026.
*   The library has raised $5 million and is seeking an additional $500,000 through a private fundraising campaign.
*   Architects Davis, Bowen and Friedel are working on the project.
*   Representatives have engaged with community members in town-hall meetings to determine the needs of the new library, which will include quiet spaces for reading, improved facilities for teens, and interactive outside spaces.

**STORY 4: Free young gardeners event at library**

*   The Talbot County Garden Club and Talbot County Free Library will host “The Young Gardeners: Flower and Plant Press Project” on March 7 at the downtown library for kids in grades 1-4 from 4 p.m. to 5 p.m.
*   The event is free, free registration is required at tcfl.org or 410-822-1626, and is hosted by the Talbot County Garden Club’s Young Gardeners Committee.

**STORY 5: Ann O'Connor**

*   Ann O'Connor, a 55-year-old Easton resident, is running for the District 2 seat on the Talbot County Board of Education as a former teacher and mother of six.
*   She prioritizes advancing academics, boosting accountability, and cultivating character.
*   The most important issue facing Talbot County Public Schools is the steady decline of academic rigor, which O'Connor aims to address through increased emphasis on traditional learning methods and early intervention in elementary schools.

**STORY 6: Girl Scouts care for furry friends**

*   Claire Anderson-Schultz, Amelia Beach, Clara Gerber and Ayesha Tapia, sixth graders from Troop 899 in Centreville, earned their Bronze Awards by helping the Animal Care Shelter for Kent County in Chestertown with a service project that included making handmade supplies and needed items for cats and dogs.
*   The girls completed 20 hours of service from June to September to meet the shelter’s needs, which included new cat tree, toys, pet food, treats, and cleaning items.
*   Paige Tucker is a shelter worker at the Animal Care Shelter in Chestertown.
*   Allison Otadan shared the shelter is grateful for the efforts of the girls.

**STORY 7: Schools struggle to bridge learning gaps, adapt to a new normal**

*   Queen Anne's County Public Schools Superintendent Patricia Saelens emphasized the district’s focus on tutoring, academic intervention, and social-emotional strategies to address learning gaps.
*   Chronic absenteeism remains above 25% across Mid-Shore counties, with Queen Anne's at 22.9%.
*   Despite setbacks, educators see lasting benefits from pandemic-related changes, including increased proficiency with technology and innovative approaches like student support coaches.
*   Students who graduated during the pandemic are now succeeding in academics and careers, proving their resilience.
*   The Mid-Shore school districts continue to navigate post-COVID realities with a focus on adaptation and social-emotional development.

**STORY 8: QACPS joins insulin litigation class action lawsuit**

*   The Queen Anne's County Public Schools Board of Education joined a lawsuit against companies for price gouging on insulin, with the district facing potential costs from a settlement.
*   Superintendent Patricia Saelens noted that the fair price of insulin is around $35 per vial.
*   The board reached a consensus to consider joining the action after a review and consultation with the school district attorney.

**STORY 9: ‘Share and make merry’: International students get a taste of American holidays**

*   International students from around the world attended a Thanksgiving dinner sponsored by the University of Delaware’s Center for Global Programs & Services on November 24, 2024.
*   Approximately 500 international students and family members joined 500 local university staff and faculty for a traditional American turkey dinner.
*   The event, now in its 11th year, aims to introduce international students to an American tradition and build community among the student body.

**STORY 10: Maple Elementary School celebrates Teacher Appreciation Week**

*   Maple Elementary School in Cambridge, MD celebrated Teacher Appreciation Week from May 7-11, 2024.
*   Principal Myyesha Ford planned activities to boost staff morale and show appreciation for teachers.
*   Activities included a “Survivor”-themed challenge wheel, learning software, health department contracts, and facilities improvements.

**STORY 17: Eastern Shore legislators discuss education funding and county burdens**

*   Eastern Shore legislators discussed education funding and county burdens on February 8, 2025.
*   Local government research manager Hiram Burch outlined how local governments will assume $144 million of state budget gaps in fiscal year 2026, including a $970,000 projection for Queen Anne's County.
*   Gov. Wes Moore’s administration touted record education funding, but many legislators questioned whether it was mandated by the state’s funding plan, The Blueprint, under former Gov. Larry Hogan.
*   Del. Sheree Sample-Hughes stated that special education funding would remain unaffected by proposed cuts to the Developmental Disabilities Administration.

**STORY 20: Caroline schools outline funding plan for new Lockerman Middle in capital improvement program**

*   Caroline County Public Schools Superintendent Derek Simmons presented the fiscal year 2027 capital improvement program to the Board of Education on September 2.
*   The plan allocates $1.4 million for Lockerman Middle School’s new location in FY27, increasing to $8.1 million in FY28 and $15.5 million in FY29.
*   Simmons expects state funding to be lowered over time due to projections.
*   The county and state will negotiate the allocation amounts, with the hope of meeting a “middle ground”.
