## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

**Story 1: Exploding pumpkins take center stage at WC - Annual event draws crowds with explosions and ooze, encouraging STEM participation**

*   Washington College hosted its annual exploding pumpkins event on October 27, 2023.
*   Event was in conjunction with the American Chemical Society Student Affiliates Club and GAMMA ETA Chemistry Honor Society.
*   This year’s experiments included glow-in-the-dark luminol, color changing acid and base paired with dry ice and liquid nitrogen.
*   Sarah Arradondo, Washington College Assistant Professor of Chemistry, attended the event.

**Story 2: Talbot school leaders discuss immediate, long-term effects of Blueprint cuts**

*   Talbot County Public Schools may face a $1.7 million funding gap in fiscal year 2025.
*   Governor Wes Moore proposed the Excellence in Maryland Public Schools Act.
*   The Act could reduce Talbot County’s funding by $685,000 in fiscal year 2026.
*   District Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi and Board Member Amy Dodson criticized the proposals.
*   The proposals cite the impact on compensatory education and multilingual learners funding.
*   Cumulative reductions of $30 million over eight years if the bill is passed.

**Story 3: Character Counts celebrates 24th anniversary**

*   Character Counts celebrated its 24th anniversary on November 12, 2024.
*   Recognition from Queen Anne’s County Commissioners.
*   The initiative was launched in fall 2000, following the Columbine tragedy.
*   Approximately 70,000 to 100,000 students have been reached.
*   Character Counts Coaches are present in 10 county pre-k classrooms and all Queen Anne’s County middle and elementary schools.
*   Over 4,200 students receive character education experiences this year.

**Story 4: What it was like ... to be a teacher**

*   Tamara Forte was a first-grade teacher in her 15th year when COVID-19 prompted changes to teaching methods and classrooms.
*   Teachers were told to close schools for two weeks, then later had to send learning packets home with limited control over their instruction.
*   Teachers adapted to virtual learning, conducting Zoom calls, troubleshooting internet issues, and sending inspiring messages to students.
*   When fall returned, teachers prepared materials for at-home use, spaced desks 6 feet apart, and simultaneously taught in-person and at-home students before rotating schedules allowed students to return to school in a limited capacity.

**Story 6: Talbot County students return to school**

*   Students at Talbot County’s eight public schools returned to classrooms on August 25, 2025.
*   The district began its school year earlier than other districts, starting after Labor Day weekend.
*   St. Michaels Elementary Principal Indra Bullock greeted families at car drop-off.
*   Teachers showed kids the way to the front door.
*   Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi announced the implementation of a cell phone pouch policy for St. Michaels Middle School students and emergency lanyards for staff.

**Story 8: Trump wiped out 75 years of science leadership**

*   Approximately 1.1 million foreign students were studying at US universities in 2023-2024, contributing $38 billion annually to the education system.
*   The Trump administration revoked student visas for thousands of international students, including Mahmoud Khalil and Rumeysa Ozturk, who were accused of peaceful protesting against the war in Gaza.
*   This may lead to a brain drain, as foreign students and researchers consider leaving due to the withdrawal of funds and academic freedom, threatening US leadership in medical science research and other sciences.
*   The US has won 72 Nobel Prizes in science since 2000, with immigrants winning about 30%.

**Story 9: St. Michaels residents donate hygiene supplies for student success**
