## Caroline County Education Beat Book - Notes

**STORY 1: 'Priceless': Students watch Maryland Supreme Court hearings at Easton High**

*   The Maryland Supreme Court held hearings at Easton High School on October 3, 2025.
*   The court consisted of seven justices.
*   The court heard two separate cases: one civil and one criminal.
*   Students from local high schools and colleges interacted with the justices through a question-and-answer session and a lunch.
*   Justice Brynja Booth spearheaded the effort, alongside Talbot County Public Schools Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi and Assistant Superintendent Helga Einhorn.
*   Opinions from the court are expected at a later date.

**STORY 3: Student-parents need support from colleges**

*   Mike Muñoz, president of Long Beach City College, emphasizes the need for colleges to support student-parents.
*   Most student-parents are mothers of color who identify as single and are facing financial disadvantages.
*   Long Beach City College offers various resources, including a Child Development Center and cost-free after-school care.
*   Plans to expand these services through partnerships like Raise The Barr and Ascend at the Aspen Institute’s Postsecondary Leadership Circle Activation Fund.

**STORY 4: Anna Howie**

*   Anna Howie, a resident of Easton and assistant principal at Federalsburg Elementary School, was appointed to the Talbot County Board of Education by Gov. Wes Moore in October 2023.
*   Her priorities include increasing student achievement in math and reading, recruiting quality teachers, and addressing the district’s fiscal responsibility.
*   Howie supports the Blueprint For Maryland's Future, citing its focus on early childhood education, diverse teachers, and resources for students’ success.
*   Her priorities align with those of Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi.

**STORY 5: Kent 4-H alumna Jill Bramble to lead National Council**

*   Jill Bramble, a Kent County 4-H alumna, has been named president and CEO of the National 4-H Council.
*   Bramble spent nearly 20 years with the organization, starting as a grant writer and rising to executive vice president and chief growth officer.

**STORY 6: Jymil Thompson resigns from Talbot County Board of Education**

*   Jymil Thompson resigned from his seat on the Talbot County Board of Education due to appointment as interim superintendent of Dorchester County Public Schools.
*   Thompson was appointed in March after serving as assistant superintendent for DCPS since September 2023.
*   Thompson's term on the board ended in December 2022, but he will serve until 2026 as interim superintendent.

**STORY 8: DCPS 'encouraged' by 2024 Maryland School Report Card**

*   The 2024 Maryland School Report Card results showed improvement in Dorchester County Public Schools.
*   North Dorchester Middle School and Mace's Lane Middle School gained stars for academic achievement.
*   Increases were seen in English language arts and math proficiency, graduation rates, and attendance.
*   The district still faces a goal to move from 23rd to 15th in academic performance statewide within four years.
*   Superintendent Jymil Thompson emphasized the need for continued focus on targeted strategies and interventions to achieve this goal.

**STORY 10: Maryland’s teacher shortage: Will the Blueprint’s plan for better pay, training do enough?**

*   Maryland's teacher shortage worsened to 28 subjects in the current school year, with gaps in English as a second language, health science, and special education.
*   The Blueprint for Maryland's Future aims to address the shortage through higher pay ($60,000 by 2026), better training, and recruitment efforts.
*   Experts Simon Birenbaum and Mike Hansen question whether higher pay alone will solve the issue.
*   The teacher shortage is partly due to declining interest in teaching careers since 2012 and an influx of less-credentialed teachers due to increased issuance of conditional certificates.

**STORY 11: CAROLINE COUNTY BOARD OF EDUCATION/ Sept. 2, 2025**

*   On September 2, 2025, the Caroline County Board of Education approved a five-year capital improvement program of $103 million and a staffing update that showed a fully staffed school system with only three instructional assistant vacancies.
*   The board also approved an overnight field trip to the National Convention and new facilities projects totaling $111,000.
*   Preston Elementary added a fishbowl window and implemented conscious discipline, while Colonel Richardson Middle School installed a new roof and implemented positive behavioral interventions and supports.

**STORY 12: St. Michaels' Classic Motor Museum donation campaign for new property**

*   The Classic Motor Museum in St. Michaels, MD is seeking $300,000 in donations to complete its $1.2 million goal for a property extension by Jan. 17, 2025.
*   The non-profit organization aims to provide hands-on experiences for students of the Automotive Technician Apprenticeship Program.
*   The program began in 2022 and has gathered over $900,000 in donations so far, with Gina Verhalen as administrator.
*   The museum plans to renovate the property to include a '50s style gas station and classroom learning space.

**STORY 14: Maryland Corps, Service Year Option graduates reflect on their experiences**

*   The state-run programs allowed participants to gain experience, crucial contacts, and learn about giving back to their communities.
*   Brad Krantz of St. Michaels secured a job at Talisman Therapeutic Riding up to $15/hour, while Emma Bush of Salisbury built skills for her future career through time management, resume building, and financial trainings.
*   The programs were launched by the state last year in partnership with Historically Black Colleges and Universities and community colleges.
