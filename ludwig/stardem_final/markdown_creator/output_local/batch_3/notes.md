**CarePacks:**

**Queen Anne's High School - College Science Partnership:**
*   Students from Queen Anne's County High School worked alongside college chemistry students at Washington College on November 14-15, 2023.
*   The partnership was developed by Michael Geoffroy and Leslie Sherman to provide high schoolers with college-level experience.
*   Students worked in modern lab facilities under the supervision of professors like Betsy Moyer-Taylor, Jeremy Bard, and Frank Keegan.

**Chesapeake College Technology Center Project:**
*   Clifford Coppersmith, President of Chesapeake College, made his case for a new technology center to the Caroline County Commission on September 5 and again at a virtual meeting with county leaders on September 18.
*   The commission agreed to support the project but expressed concerns about future costs associated with the 2021 Blueprint for Maryland’s Future and debt.
*   Commission President Travis Breeding stated he was "on the fence" with the proposal, citing concerns about taking on more debt and hampering future boards’ control over funding.
*   Talbot and Queen Anne's counties have agreed to help finance the project, but Dorchester County declined and Kent County had not made a decision as of September 18.
*   The commission will make a final decision on September 26.

**Maryland State Department of Education - Literacy Policy:**
*   The Maryland State Department of Education presented a draft literacy policy to stakeholders on July 23, led by Deputy State Superintendent Deann Collins and Executive Director Tenette Smith.
*   The proposed policy aims to address achievement gaps in literacy, particularly among students from minority groups living in poverty.
*   Research suggests that early reading proficiency is critical for long-term academic success and future opportunities.
*   The comprehensive policy draft includes four components: teacher support, assessment and parent notification, instruction and intervention, and retention and intervention.

**Stephanie Jovine - Childcare Search:**
*   Stephanie Jovine searched for child care in Maryland in 2015 and found two bad options; she finally found a suitable program at LiLi's Child Care Center in Temple Hills after six months of searching.
*   Maryland offers a rating system to help parents select child care facilities, but providers say it is difficult to navigate.
*   Many families struggle with the state’s scholarship program, which can have long wait times and complex requirements.
*   Jovine discovered the program too late for her first child, Lily, born in February 2023, and initially had to pay out of pocket; she later received temporary aid through the program’s fast-track option.
*   A new fast-track program aims to reduce wait times, but providers complain about delays in receiving scholarship payments.

**Maryland’s Education ‘Blueprint’ Struggles to Expand Pre-K:**
*   Maryland’s pre-K program struggles to expand due to a lack of private providers and inadequate physical space in schools.
*   The state’s "Blueprint" plan aims to make pre-K free for low- and moderate-income families and available to all 3- and 4-year-olds, but faces challenges in finding enough private providers.
*   Many districts are struggling to meet the requirement of having 35% of pre-K seats filled by private providers, and some child care centers lack the necessary funding and space to participate.
*   The state’s physical space requirements for pre-K classrooms have also been criticized as too inflexible.

**Washington College - Hodson Trust Gift:**
*   Washington College received a $54.7 million gift from The Hodson Trust, increasing its scholarship endowment to $170 million and overall endowment to $325 million.
*   President Mike Sosulski announced that the gift guarantees minimum scholarships of $30,000 per year for students with at least a 3.3 GPA.
*   The trust’s support will strengthen access to education for students from all backgrounds, particularly first-generation or underserved communities.

**Chapel District Elementary - Renovation Project:**
*   Chapel District Elementary School in Cordova, Maryland is set to welcome students next week after completing its renovations.
*   The $27 million project includes adding 16,000 square feet and will be fully finished by 2026.
*   Construction teams completed the second phase of the project this summer, including the school cafeteria and some classrooms, led by Whiting-Turner Contracting Company.
*   Principal Kari Clow reported a smooth staff return earlier in the week.
*   The school’s gym is not ready for opening and will reopen by September.
