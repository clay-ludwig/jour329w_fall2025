## Caroline County Education Beat Book - Notes (December 14, 2025)

**Story 1: Anti-war protests, rises in antisemitism magnify challenges for free speech on college campuses**

*   Anti-war protests and rising antisemitism are raising challenges for free speech on college campuses.
*   The Anti-Defamation League reports a rise in antisemitism since Oct. 7.
*   The Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression argues that students' First Amendment rights are being restricted.
*   The U.S. Department of Education is investigating Title VI violations at over 40 colleges, including Johns Hopkins University, Harvard University, and the University of Michigan.
*   Shira Goodman and Jane Kirtley express concerns about government and donor influence on speech.

**Story 2: KCHS valedictorian codes bus program for families**

*   Ben Loller, Kent County High School's Class of 2024 valedictorian and Brown University-bound student, created a coding project to track students' school buses using web-based software and location transmission hardware.
*   Loller served on the Board of Education's Citizen Advisory Committee and raised the idea for such a program after discussing it with other parents and committee members.
*   The project was developed in collaboration with William Poore, Kent County Public Schools' supervisor of information technology, as part of the Makerspace program.
*   Loller presented his work to the Kent County Board of Education on May 28, 2024, and received praise from board members and school administrators.
*   Loller plans to continue working on the project at Brown University in Rhode Island this fall.

**Story 3: Family holding out hope for turf field project at Easton High**

*   Turf field project at Easton High School uncertain.
*   Elgin Hawley's wish was granted with Make-A-Wish Mid-Atlantic support in July 2023.
*   $1.5 million allocated for outstanding capital projects, including turf field, which has an estimated price of $1.8 million.
*   Talbot County Public Schools received funding from the county and made a commitment to partner with Make-A-Wish.
*   Elgin Hawley's aneurysm led her to wish for a turf field; she graduated this spring after returning to school in February 2022.

**Story 4: Ravens flock; Local kids honored at NFL game**

*   The Kent Island High School Interact Club was honored by the Baltimore Ravens with the National Football League team's Honor Rows distinction on October 13.
*   The award recognized their commitment to community service and leadership development, led by teacher Lacey Phelps and in partnership with the Giving the Edge Foundation.
*   The Leadership Cohort program started in 2021 at Kent Island High School and has grown to over 40+ students who volunteer before school to discuss their passions.
*   On November 22, 2024, nearly 50 students attended a Ravens game against the Washington Commanders and were celebrated for their contributions.

**Story 6: Ed Spec Committee recommends North Caroline site for new Lockerman Middle**

*   Caroline County Board of Education recommends North Caroline High School site for new Lockerman Middle School due to infrastructure advantages and shared spaces for events.
*   The proposed location is expected to save $500,000 in development costs compared to Double Hills Road site.
*   The new middle school project budget would be $103.82 million, with state funding covering around $80 million and local funding the remainder.

**Story 7: Caroline County Board of Ed declares last day of school, discuss virtual instruction**

*   The Caroline County Board of Education declared June 17 as the last day of school due to inclement weather closures exceeding four days allotted.
*   The board concluded that conducting virtual instruction on inclement weather days would be challenging and likely unfeasible.
*   Factors considered include ensuring student internet access, providing necessary devices and translation services for multilingual learners, special education students, and homeless students.
*   The estimated cost to provide the required devices was approximately $736,000 for students and $35,000 for teachers over five years.

**Story 16: Lockerman Middle Receives STEM Donation**

**Story 18: Thank you for your service**

*   Michael Feehley and Tom Carlson, two veterans, put up dozens of flags at the Easton Elks Lodge on May 26, 2024.
*   Feehley teaches schools about the sacrifices made by veterans since the Korean War, imparting the message "All gave some and some gave all."
*   He also tells students that veterans' families often went without during their service, reminding them not to take liberties for granted.
*   Feehley's program aims to promote gratitude towards veterans through a teach-in at schools, with decorated veteran Tom Carlson helping to hoist flags.

**Story 19: Decline in social capital has reached new lows**

*   Robert Putnam's 1995 book "Bowling Alone" analyzed the decline in social capital in American society starting from 1950.
*   The author suggests this decline has worsened due to limited social interactions during the Covid-19 pandemic, particularly among young children.
*   A recent incident at a GOP dinner in Talbot County highlights the need for renewed civic engagement and collaboration.
*   Putnam's book and upcoming programs, such as the Chesapeake Forum and Baylor University webinar, offer opportunities for citizens to learn about and improve social capital.
