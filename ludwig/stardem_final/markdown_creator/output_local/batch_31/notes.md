## Caroline County Public Schools - Notes

*   **Superintendent Simmons & Budget:** Dr. Derek Simmons, Superintendent of Caroline County Public Schools, outlined budget priorities and timeline for fiscal year 2025 due to uncertain Blueprint demands and decreasing pandemic-related grant funding.
*   **Board Approval of Timeline:** The Board of Education approved a revised timeline to move Simmons’s final budget proposal from February to April.
*   **Dual Enrollment Challenges:** Key challenges include meeting dual enrollment requirements and starting teacher salaries, as well as decreasing state and federal funding.
*   **2023 School Improvements:** Caroline County Public Schools Department of Operations personnel spruced up buildings and grounds over summer 2023, including installing smoke and vapor sensors in all secondary schools, refinishing high school gym floors with new mascot logos, remodeling cafeterias, and replacing roofs at Federalsburg Elementary School and North Caroline High School. Director of Operations William Mengel presented a slide show featuring the projects completed at the Board of Education’s Sept. 5 meeting.
*   **Lockerman Middle School Committee:** The Lockerman Middle School Committee heard and addressed concerns at its second meeting on June 3, 2025. Architect Robyn Toth confirmed that the current building cannot be renovated due to state assessment results, and it will become a community center. The committee presented two new location options for a new school: one behind North Caroline High School and the other on Double Hills Road in Denton.
*   **Cell Phone Pouch Pilot Program:** Caroline County Public Schools will bring back cellphone pouches in middle schools for a second year, following a successful pilot program introduced in the 2024-25 school year. The company Yondr created the pouches, and they were tested in both Caroline County middle schools last year. The first year saw 30.6% fewer referrals, 130 fewer attacks or fights, and 200 fewer instances of disrespect and disruptions. Superintendent Derek Simmons said student and parent receptivity improved over time, with minimal resistance to the program's compliance.
