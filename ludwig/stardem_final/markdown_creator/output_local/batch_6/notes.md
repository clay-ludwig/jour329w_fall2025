## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

**Story 1: Target team will not see expansion this season**

*   Kent Island Clay Target Team will not see expansion this season.
*   Superintendent Patricia Saelens noted that Queen Anne's County was the only public school district in Maryland offering clay target teams.
*   Team limit remains as it is.
*   Team is self-funded and participates at a private facility.
*   Currently has six all-volunteer coaches.
*   Shawn Connolly, head coach, requested approval to expand the student limit, but was denied by the board.

**Story 2: Talbot Board of Ed approves funding for capital projects, discusses turf field status**

*   Talbot Board of Education approved funding for capital projects.
*   Easton High auditorium lights ($817,321)
*   St. Michaels Middle/High School LED lights ($566,000)
*   $117,000 allocated to athletic field projects in general.
*   Talbot County Public Schools has $1.5 million in fiscal year 2025 funding from the county for capital projects.
*   Board discussed the potential for an artificial turf field at Easton High, but no official motion was made due to concerns over safety and prioritization.

**Story 3: Candidates have varying views**

*   2024 election season in Talbot County will choose school board representatives.
*   Six candidates with varying views on education.
*   Conservative candidates: Ann O’Connor, Karla Wieland-Cherry, Kerry Gunshenan.
*   Progressive candidates: Cynthia Jurrius, Anna Howie, Candace Henry.
*   Moms for Liberty hosted a debate featuring four of the six candidates.
*   Avalon Theater hosted a separate debate with the remaining two progressive candidates.
*   Talbot County Democratic Forum sponsored another debate with the two conservative candidates invited to speak.

**Story 4: More than half of Talbot County students receive free and reduced lunches**

*   Over half of Talbot County Public Schools students receive free and reduced lunches.
*   Rates as high as 76% at Easton Elementary School.
*   Food insecurity is on the rise in Talbot County, where costs of food, housing, and other necessities outpace salary growth.
*   Maryland Department of Education found a 13% increase in free and reduced meals from the previous school year to 2022-2023.
*   Gov. Wes Moore signed a state Senate bill requiring reporting of costs to provide universal free school meals to all public school students in Maryland.

**Story 5: Cruise benefits St. Michaels library expansion**

*   Friends of Talbot County Free Library sponsored a cruise event on October 12 to launch the capital campaign for the St. Michaels Library expansion.
*   Over $6,000 was raised for new books.
*   Library expected to complete expansion by 2025.
*   Event featured music from AMPERSAND, gourmet appetizers and drinks, and a raffle with local business gift cards and a museum family membership.
*   Fall Book Sale will take place on October 27 at the Easton library, followed by pre-sale opportunities on October 26 and book donations accepted on October 24.

**Story 6: Dorchester County saw some big gains**

*   Dorchester County saw the largest jump in English proficiency among Maryland school districts during the 2024-25 school year.
*   District ranks 19th out of 24 in English proficiency.
*   Superintendent Jymil Thompson attributed the improvement as progress towards climbing state rankings.

**Story 7: Freedom of speech under assault on campuses**

*   Columbia University expelled, suspended, and revoked degrees of students accused of peaceful protest against pro-Palestinian speech in 2025.
*   Trump administration rescinded $400 million in federal funding for such speech.
*   President George H.W. Bush launched Operation Desert Storm in 1991, leading to the bombing and slaughter of retreating Iraqi troops and civilians.
*   US has continued to wage war since then, with the current Gaza genocide protests sparking debate over free speech.

**Story 8: Foundation of HOPE's new building to expand middle school girls program**

*   Foundation of HOPE plans to build a 5,200-square-foot, two-story building in Easton, MD on South Washington Street for its middle school girls program.
*   Project valued at $2.2 million.
*   Keasha Haythe, founder and president of the organization, believes the new building will improve the experience for students, including increased accessibility and more resources.
*   Construction could begin as soon as September 2025.

**Story 9: Caroline students sign apprenticeship agreements**

*   Caroline County Public Schools signed four youth apprenticeship agreements with local businesses: three in the Technology Department and one in the Chesapeake Culinary Arts Center.
*   Students involved: Evamarie Vargas Villacorta, Dana Velasquez-Cifuentes, Lawrence Johnson, Wilson Clough.
*   CCPS Superintendent Dr. Derek Simmons led the signing, which he described as an "exciting day" made possible by former Assistant Superintendent Milton Nagel's vision.
*   Representatives from the Maryland State Department of Education attended to support the program.

**Story 10: Easton flying event gets women up in the air**

*   Maryland Chapter of the Ninety-Nines hosted the first-ever Let's Fly Now event at Easton Airport on September 14, 2024.
*   21 women, including Maria Esparraguera and Cheryl Mohrmann, piloted two flights.
*   Event aimed to encourage more women to fly planes.
*   Attendees ranged from local teens to a woman who drove from Brooklyn, New York.
*   Event well-received, with attendees showing enthusiasm for flying and interest in future events.
