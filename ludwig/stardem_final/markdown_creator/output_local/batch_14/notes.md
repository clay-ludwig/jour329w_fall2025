## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

*   **Counties Ponder Money for College Building:** Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Talbot, and Queen Anne’s counties are considering contributing $79 million for a new Technical Center at Chesapeake College. The Maryland Higher Education Commission will review letters of intent by September 28. The state will cover 75% of the project’s cost, with each county agreeing to provide the remaining 25%. Construction is set for 2026 and will be located next to the Eastern Shore Higher Education Center.

*   **Easton Middle School Drops a Star in Maryland School Report Card:** Easton Middle School lost a star in the 2024 Maryland School Report Card, dropping from three stars and 48th percentile to two stars and 32nd percentile. The school scored 44 points, down from 46.1 in 2023. Debbie Gardner, Talbot County Public Schools’ Director of Communications, stated that the district is “closely examining” the report card results and sees opportunities for data collection and analysis.

*   **Chapel District Elementary Renovations Nearing 30% Completion Mark:** Construction teams at Chapel District Elementary School are making progress on renovations that began in summer 2024 and are expected to take about two years to complete. The project has spent around $11.28 million, or 30% of its total cost, with approximately $27 million in county funding and state funds from Maryland's Interagency Commission on School Construction. Director Kevin Shafer noted the construction was started 34 days late due to septic system issues, but credits project manager Bill Stagg for getting the project back on track. The school’s renovations will add around 16,000 square feet of learning space and include a new media center and playground renovation.

*   **DCPS Approves Fiscal Year 2025 Budget in 3 to 1 Vote:** DCPS approved fiscal year 2025 budget with a 3-1 vote on June 3, despite concerns raised by some board members and community residents. The $88.5 million operating budget includes a $7.4 million increase from the previous year, primarily due to state revenue sources. Dorchester County Public Schools Interim Superintendent Jymil Thompson made changes to the budget, including redistributing media specialists to other positions in the school system. Board member Chris Wheedleton expressed concerns about the lack of community feedback and process, while others defended the decision, citing financial constraints and the need for cost savings.

*   **Radcliffe Creek School Awarded 'Social-Emotional' Learning Grant:** Radcliffe Creek School was awarded a grant from the Queen Anne’s County Mental Health Committee to expand its social-emotional learning curriculum. The grant will bring additional curriculums, Brain Talk and Conscious Discipline, to older students and their families. Radcliffe Creek School Head of School Peter Thayer said the funding will help meet the needs of neurodivergent students. The QACMHC President Kelly Phipps credited community support for the grant, which will also bring social-emotional programming to the local community starting in 2024.

*   **Stronger Defense, Improved Education:** National defense and youth education are being overlooked by politicians, despite being long-term concerns. The latest national defense budget proposes reduced military readiness spending, while interest payments on federal debt may soon surpass defense spending. Meanwhile, increased public funding for education has not led to improving academic outcomes, threatening global competitiveness and domestic innovation.

*   **KCPS Calendar Approved with Post Labor Day Start:** The Kent County Board of Education approved a calendar for the 2024-25 school year with a post-Labor Day start. The new calendar includes 180 instruction days and two inclement weather days. It begins on Tuesday, Sept. 3, with staggered starts for different grade levels, including: first through sixth grades and ninth-graders (Sept. 3), 10th, 11th, and 12th grades (Sept. 4), and pre-kindergartners and kindergartners (Sept. 5).

*   **Aiming to Fly: Civil Air Patrol Holds Open House for Young, Aspiring Pilots:** The Civil Air Patrol held an open house at the St. Mary’s County Regional Airport on August 28 to introduce young potential members to its cadet program, which offers career exploration in aviation and military service. The program is open to members aged 12-21 and provides opportunities for flying, search and rescue, community service, and wilderness survival training. Local residents attended the event, including parents and children who expressed interest in joining the program after learning about its values and activities.

*   **CBMM Summer Camp Registration Opens Jan. 2 for Members:** CBMM summer camp registration opens on Jan. 2, 2025, with a two-week sign-up period exclusively for members. Registration for eight weeks of camps begins on Jan. 16, and will be open to non-members thereafter. CBMM offers various camps, including Sea Squirts (ages 4-6), Terrapins (grades 1-3), Summer Workshop (grades 4-6 & 6-9), Museum Masters (grades 4-6), and Inspiration Chesapeake (grades 6-9) camps, with tuition ranging from $165 to $375 per week.

*   **Olde School Builders Welcomes Student Apprentice:** Olde School Builders welcomed Kimberly Escalante-Garcia as its first apprentice on June 16, 2025. Escalante-Garcia, a rising senior at North Caroline High School, was chosen after showing initiative to pursue the apprenticeship after attending a Junior Achievement event.

*   **'I am speechless:' TCPS Awards Teacher, Support Staff of the Year:** Rosario Steele was awarded Talbot County Public Schools Support Staff of the Year in 2024-2025, along with Jeremy Hillyard, who won the district’s Teacher of the Year honor. Hillyard has taught at Easton High School since 2010 and is working to create a mentorship program for Hispanic students. Steele works as an instructional assistant for English as a second language students at EHS.
