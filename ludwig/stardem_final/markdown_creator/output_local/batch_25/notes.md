## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

**STORY 1: HopStart learners making world a better place**

*   The 24th annual HopStart new venture awards program took place in May 2024.
*   Hosted by the Center for Leadership Education at Johns Hopkins.
*   Len Foxwell, a member of the Hopkins team planning and executing HopStart, attended.
*   Program categories included general ventures and medical technology/life science ventures.
*   50 submissions were received with ideas ranging from leveraging internet use for agriculture to simplifying credential verification processes for medical care providers.

**STORY 2: Classmate's legacy lives on in Kent Island**

*   Darcy Lynn, Kent Island High School’s Class of 2024 valedictorian, spoke at the graduation ceremony on May 30, 2024.
*   Lynn honored classmate Jacob Sloan, who died in their seventh grade year.
*   Lynn encouraged her peers to carry on Sloan’s legacy of kindness by making the world a better place through everyday actions.
*   Lynn quoted Taylor Swift’s advice to “be good to people.”
*   Lynn congratulated her classmates and urged them to embrace their future with kindness and community spirit.

**STORY 3: Forum notable for absent candidates**

*   Four candidates – Candace Henry, Ann O’Connor, Anna Howie, and Cynthia Jurrius – attended a Community Town Hall event in Easton, MD on October 5, 2024.
*   The event was notable for the absence of other candidates.

**STORY 4: Caroline County school athletic facilities will soon have bathrooms**

*   Caroline County Commissioners President Travis Breeding and Public Schools Director of Operations Bill Mengel worked together to add new bathrooms to North Caroline High School and Colonel Richardson High School outdoor athletic facilities.
*   The project was included in the county’s fiscal year 2025 budget, which was approved by commissioners.
*   The new bathrooms will have separate men’s and women’s sides with toilets at both high schools.
*   Expected to be operational by late September or early October.

**STORY 5: St. Michaels library to feature tax stamps**

*   Ron Lesher, a stamp collector and judge, will speak at the St. Michaels Branch Library on October 2 about “The Taxation of Legal Alcohol During National Prohibition, 1920-1933.”
*   The event is sponsored by the Friends of the Library.
*   Coffee and dessert will be provided.

**STORY 6: MACo's Blueprint panelists stress importance of local context at session in Cambridge**

*   Garrett County Blueprint Coordinator Nicole Miller emphasized the importance of local context during a session at the Maryland Association of Counties Winter Conference in Cambridge.
*   Panelists, including Christina Miller and Derek Simmons, recommended revisiting the Blueprint due to funding challenges and inequities in implementation.
*   St. Mary’s County Commissioner Mike Hewitt expressed concern about raising county taxes for the Blueprint.
*   Sen. Mary Beth Carozza stated that Gov. Wes Moore is willing to make adjustments to the plan.

**STORY 7: Talbot Board of Education candidates speak at town hall forum**

*   Candidates for Talbot County Board of Education fielded questions during an open forum on October 4, 2024, discussing standardized test scores, student engagement and teacher retention.
*   Incumbents Candace Henry, Ann O’Connor, Anna Howie, and Cynthia Jurrius attended the forum.
*   Scores showed slight improvements in math and English proficiency rates, but all candidates agreed that test scores must improve.
*   The District 6 seat is a race between Kerry Gunshenan and Cynthia Jurrius.
*   The school board candidates will be on the ballot on November 5, 2024.

**STORY 8: Maryland school district delves into AI, conduct codes, cheating**

*   Cecelia Lewis, director of student services, presented proposed changes to Calvert County public school system’s code of conduct, including guidelines for the use of artificial intelligence (AI).
*   The proposed code includes definitions of “academic dishonesty/cheating” that now include unauthorized use of AI-generated materials and utilizing AI to harass or bully.
*   Board members discussed plans for AI training for teachers and students, with an artificial intelligence course for teachers being developed.
*   The revised code will be posted online for public review until late June.
*   The Patuxent High School theatre group, the Harlequins, received a citation from the school board for earning superior scores at the 2025 Maryland Thespians Festival.

**STORY 9: Birds, the Brain and Plato kick off Chesapeake Forum’s fall schedule**

*   Chesapeake Forum launches its fall term on September 6 with a four-session course on “Plato’s Republic” by Forest Hansen, Ph.D., and two sessions on birding at Poplar Island led by Kristina Motley and Wayne Bell.
*   The organization also offers a one-session course titled “Healthy Living, Healthy Brain” starting September 7, taught by Jane Bateman.

**STORY 10: Young runners recognized by Board of Education**

*   The Caroline County Board of Education recognized young runners from Greensboro Elementary for their achievements, including two running clubs that ran a 5k in Cambridge on May 4, raising $4,287 for Parkinson’s awareness.

**STORY 11: Student designs school district holiday card**

*   Summer Smith's artwork was chosen for the annual Caroline County Public Schools holiday card.
*   The artwork was unveiled at a Dec. 5 board of education meeting and resulted in Summer being presented with a certificate by Superintendent Derek Simmons.
*   Smith’s design featured intricate details such as footsteps leading from a snowman to a cabin, smoke rising from the chimney, and script in the trees.

**STORY 12: Talbot Board of Education passes FY2025 budget**

*   The Talbot County Public Schools Board of Education approved the FY2025 budget, which increases the unrestricted operating budget by $7.9 million to $79.9 million.
*   The district is seeking a 3.4% increase due to decreased federal funding and losing $4.8 million in COVID-19 related grant funding.
*   A new virtual synchronous instruction service will be offered for middle and high school students who require home and hospital services or cannot attend in person due to health reasons.
*   The Edmentum agreement was approved, with costs estimated at $200 per student per course, totaling around $100,000.

**STORY 13: Dorchester schools prepare for more cuts**

*   Dorchester County Public Schools face federal funding cuts of over $642,000 due to Trump Administration’s decision to withhold $6.8 billion in education funding.
*   The cuts impact teacher training, internal and external student experiences, and English Language Learner programs.
*   Dorchester County faced a budget deficit of $10.19 million in January, which was reduced to $5.59 million by March 2025.
