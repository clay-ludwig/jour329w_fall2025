## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

*   **Constitution Bowl Contest:** The Mid-Shore Constitution Alliance hosts the U.S. Constitution Scholastic Competition on Sept. 20 at the Talbot County Free Library in partnership with the Colonel Tench Tilghman Chapter of the Sons of the American Revolution. Competing teams from local schools earn community recognition and prizes.
*   **Caregiver Scholarship:** Casey Hart, a Worton resident, won the $3,000 Jean Griswold Home Care National Caregiver Scholarship to pursue an RN nursing degree. Hart works for Griswold Home Care in suburban Philadelphia, nominated due to her exceptional caregiving work in Kent County. Dr. Jean Griswold founded Griswold Home Care over 40 years ago, and the Jean Griswold Foundation offers financial support to caregivers.
*   **Cyber Capital Funding:** Gov. Wes Moore announced a $1.8 million award to Maryland’s 16 community colleges to boost cybersecurity training and career opportunities. The funding comes from the Accelerating Cyber Careers grant by the Maryland Department of Labor and will be provided by BCR Cyber, a private company offering cybersecurity training and job placement services.
*   **Blueprint Accountability Board Concerns:** The Blueprint Accountability and Implementation Board is concerned about inadequate funding for Maryland’s education system, including shortfalls in classroom essentials, interventions for struggling learners, and career and technology education. The governor and General Assembly have not addressed these issues. The board can recommend legislative changes and track compliance.
*   **Talbot County Classrooms:** Talbot County classrooms are in session with a new school year bringing fresh opportunities for education coverage.
*   **Diving Deep with Dyslexia:** Robin Williams, Jay Leno, and other famous figures had dyslexia. Lindsey Parte, a Special Education teacher in Stevensville, Maryland, reignited the Decoding Dyslexia Maryland Eastern Shore Chapter to raise awareness about dyslexia, especially for children. Parte’s son Lincoln was diagnosed with dyslexia at age 8. The Mayo Clinic states that dyslexia is a learning disorder involving difficulty decoding speech sounds and letters.
*   **Caroline 2025-26 School Year:** Caroline County Board of Education members voted for a post Labor Day start date for the 2025-2026 school year. The first day of school is set for Tuesday, September 2, 2025, with teachers returning to school on August 25. The county has three inclement weather days accounted for in the new calendar, following four used last year for snow.
*   **Kaitlyn Wright Finalist:** Kaitlyn Wright, a Kent County Middle School teacher, is among seven finalists for Maryland Teacher of the Year. She was previously named Kent County Public Schools' Teacher of the Year last spring and will undergo an interview process in September with a panel of state judges. The next Maryland Teacher of the Year will be announced in October 2024.
*   **Shore Christian Homeschool Graduates:** Students from Shore Christian Fellowship in Queenstown, MD, were awarded diplomas on May 18, 2024. The Class of 2024 consists of 10 graduates, including Cody Blunt, Ashlyn Boyles, Alyssa Jastram, and others. Homeschool commencement programs have been held at the church for over a decade; this year's ceremony was attended by Pastor Tom Chandler, who congratulated the graduates' parents on their commitment to education.
