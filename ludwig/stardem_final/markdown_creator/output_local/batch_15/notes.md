## Caroline County Education Notes - December 14, 2025

**Story 1: Moore says budget will balance, protect school funding**

*   Gov. Wes Moore unveiled a $63.1 billion budget proposal on January 18, 2024.
*   The budget balances state’s books and protects funding for Maryland public schools.
*   The plan prioritizes public safety, affordable housing, child care, education, and public service.
*   Moore crafted the budget with lawmakers, community leaders, and other stakeholders, aiming to make it a “reflection of our shared aspiration.”

**Story 3: High school graduation rate reaches seven-year high**

*   Maryland’s high school graduation rate reached 88% in 2024, its highest point since 2017.
*   The increase is significant among student groups that faced barriers to graduation, including Hispanic students and multilingual learners.
*   Talbot County had a graduation rate of 95%.
*   Caroline and Dorchester counties fell below the state average.
*   The Blueprint for Maryland’s Future education reform law aims to close equity gaps.

**Story 13: Caroline County Public Schools aims to expand summer education program next year**

*   Caroline County Public Schools aims to expand their summer education program next year, aiming to teach 400 more students.
*   The 2023 program attracted 916 students.
*   Superintendent Dr. Derek Simmons praised director Lindsey McCormick’s leadership.
*   The program boosted middle school numbers and provided career exploration opportunities for high school students.
*   The program saw an increase in participation from students with disabilities.

**Story 14: Moms For Liberty advocates for school parents**

*   Moms For Liberty Talbot chapter chair Jan Greenhawk responds to an article by Executive Editor Jim Johnson.
*   Greenhawk states that her group supports candidates in local school board elections.
*   Greenhawk corrects Johnson, saying Moms For Liberty did not endorse any candidates and has not raised questions across the country.
*   Greenhawk emphasizes the group’s dedication to parental rights and positive dialogue with local education officials, including Superintendent Dr. Pepukayi and school board President Emily Jackson.

**Story 15: Talbot County Free Library endures as community linchpin**

*   Talbot County Free Library opened in Easton on October 15, 1925, with an 800-book collection.
*   The library has adapted to societal changes over the years.
*   The library now serves residents across the county with over 130,000 books, computer access, and free programs.
*   Executive Director Dana Newman believes the library will continue to evolve and serve the community’s needs for years to come.
