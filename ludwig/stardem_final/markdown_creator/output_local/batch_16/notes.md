## Education Beat Book - Caroline County, Maryland - December 14, 2025

**Story 1: Chrome City Ride raises $105K for Benedictine School**

*   The Chrome City Ride raised $105,000 for Benedictine School on July 28, 2023.
*   Over 2,100 participants from six rally points across Maryland and Delaware participated.
*   The 18th annual event supports more than 200 children and adults with disabilities served by the school.
*   Benedictine School was founded in 2001.
*   The school serves students from MD, DE, WV, PA, and VA.
*   Benedictine School received a two-year accreditation for its special education programs.
*   The ride has raised over $2 million to date.

**Story 2: Talbot County resident named to Maryland Humanities board**

*   Jill Ferris, an Easton resident and vice president of education and interpretation at the Chesapeake Bay Maritime Museum, was named to Maryland Humanities' Board of Directors on November 3, 2023.
*   She joins nineteen other volunteers in service.
*   Ferris has experience as a teacher in Queen Anne’s County and received the Fulbright Fellowship and Maryland History Day award.
*   Ferris will serve alongside members from various fields and institutions.
*   Ferris supports Maryland Humanities' initiatives to connect people, enhance lives, and enrich communities through experiences exploring shared stories.

**Story 3: Black and Hispanic faculty underrepresented in universities**

*   Black and Hispanic faculty are underrepresented at Maryland's six largest public universities.
*   Less than 6% of total faculty are Black and less than 5% are Hispanic as of 2021.
*   Faculty demographics vary widely across universities.
*   A new education code instituted in 2022 requires public universities to ensure that faculty demographics reflect the state's diverse population.
*   Academia faces a multifaceted issue of underrepresentation, including hiring, onboarding, retention, and promotion.
*   The gap between student representation and faculty representation has widened, with 60:1 ratios of Black students to Black faculty members at some universities.
*   Tenured professors are predominantly white, with fewer faculty of color pursuing tenure.
*   Initiatives such as intentional mentoring policies and best practices for increasing diversity in STEM fields aim to address these issues.

**Story 4: Phone pouches improving student focus, discipline in Caroline, district says**

*   Caroline County Public Schools implemented an experimental pilot program using Yondr's cellphone pouches to keep students off their phones during school hours.
*   The first semester showed pleasing results.
*   Increased attentiveness was observed.
*   Reduced cyberbullying incidents (21% reduction) occurred.
*   Improved pass rates were noted.
*   The program aims to expand to ninth graders in future years.
*   Officials will weigh the pros and cons before making a decision.
*   Some parents have expressed concerns about not having direct communication with their children.
*   Students have tried to tamper with or break into their pouches.

**Story 5: Men For Change recognizes volunteers' service**

*   Men For Change recognized six individuals, including Brandy James, Stephanie Brohawn, William Eric Smith, Cynthia Fletcher, Cederick Turner, and Roberta Butler, for their work in mentoring youth and community service.
*   The event was held on July 20 at the Federalsburg Volunteer Fire Company banquet hall.
*   Vaughn Evans Sr. was the keynote speaker.
*   The organization’s theme this year was “Let’s Get Busy” and aims to support young people with resources to become successful leaders.
*   Men For Change is partnering with the Federalsburg Activity Center for an after-school mentoring program, which is expected to open by September.

**Story 6: Test data shows areas for growth in Talbot schools**

*   Talbot County school officials analyzed student test data and identified areas where improvement is needed.
*   The results showed some gains, but the county fell behind other counties last year.
*   Board members expressed concerns about the results, with one admitting it kept them up at night.

**Story 7: Jennifer Stanley steps down from Oxford Kids Camp**

*   Jennifer Stanley stepped down as founder and director of Oxford Kids Camp after over 40 years, leaving behind a legacy in the community.
*   She established the camp with a focus on biking, nature, and outdoor activities in 1982.
*   The camp served up to 250 kids per week.
*   Stanley’s successor is Elizabeth Wainwright, who has been part of the camp since she was a camper at age five.

**Story 9: Lockerman High School celebrates 95th anniversary**

*   Lockerman High School celebrated its 95th anniversary on July 29, 2025.

*   Lockerman High School started in 1930.

**Story 10: Done for the children**

*   Barbara Lee Jefferson's therapy dogs, all female rottweilers, were approved for Alliance of Therapy Dogs and Crisis Response Canines in 2012.
*   She has over 1,000 visits under her belt, earning National Recognition for their therapy work.
*   In August, she was told by a teacher that dogs were no longer allowed at Easton Elementary School due to a “policy change”, but the decision came without update or explanation.

**Story 11: Talbot Board of Ed discusses dress code, school uniforms**

*   The Talbot County Board of Education discussed dress code updates and the possibility of implementing school uniforms during its virtual meeting on February 14, 2025.
*   Superintendent Sharon Pepukayi revised the district's administrative regulations to remove specific language targeting certain clothing items, citing discrepancies in enforcing dress code standards.
*   Board Member Ann O’Connor expressed support for implementing school uniforms, saying they would “put everyone on the same playing field” and solve problems.
*   Board Vice President Amy Dodson mentioned her positive experience with uniforms at a past high school.
*   The board plans to gather community input and historical context before making a decision on implementing school uniforms.

**Story 12: Caroline schools closed, Talbot and Dorchester remote Wednesday**

*   Talbot and Dorchester county school campuses will be closed on February 12 due to inclement weather, with students attending school virtually.
*   Caroline County schools are also closing on February 12, but will not have a virtual learning day.
