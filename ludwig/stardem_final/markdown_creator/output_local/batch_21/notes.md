## Caroline County Education Beat Book - Notes

**STORY 1: Tyranny Of The Loud At Washington College**

*   Dr. Robert George, a conservative professor at Princeton University, was disrupted during a presentation at Washington College in Chestertown on September 21, 2023.
*   Protesters, who were not engaged in civil dialogue, marched into the room and shouted from the start, breaking up George’s talk.
*   The event ended prematurely with George being escorted from the room.
*   Campus security failed to intervene in a timely manner.
*   Washington College’s core values of integrity, civility, and free speech are at risk due to the protesters’ behavior.

**STORY 3: Community school leaders in Talbot County aim to make a difference**

*   Katie Fox and Shelby Parks lead community schools at Easton Elementary and Easton Middle in Talbot County, MD, respectively, providing resources for students in need since 2019.
*   The two schools aim to increase availability of healthy foods, provide mental health resources, and add enrichment programs, despite uncertainty over future funding due to the state’s $3 billion deficit.
*   Easton Elementary received "per-pupil" funding in its third year, supporting before- and after-school programs.
*   Easton Middle is focusing on mental health, food insecurity, and transportation challenges.
*   The district is moving forward with implementing community school programs, including partnering with Choptank Community Health.

**STORY 4: New Beginnings Youth and Family Services honors Theresa Stafford with name change**

*   New Beginnings Youth and Family Services will be renamed Dr. Theresa Stafford’s New Beginnings Learning Academy in honor of its late executive director.
*   The name change was made possible by donations from individuals who wanted to preserve the legacy of Theresa Stafford, who served as executive director until her death in December 2023.
*   Phil Feldman donated $25,000 to help secure the name change.
*   A ribbon cutting and dedication ceremony will be held on November 30 at noon to unveil the center’s new sign.
*   The event coincides with what would have been Stafford's 71st birthday and aims to celebrate her legacy and continue creating opportunities for children.

**STORY 5: Sending Shelly home**

*   Shelly, a diamondback terrapin, was part of the Terrapin Program through Maryland Environmental Service and spent her first year growing at Kent County Middle School in Chestertown.
*   In May 2024, Shelly rode on a school bus to Tilghman Island in Talbot County and then back to Poplar Island, where she was released into the wild after being part of a research program.
*   The researchers aim to determine if human involvement in terrapins' early stages gives them a better chance of survival in the nature world, as part of the Paul S. Sarbanes Ecosystem Restoration Project.
*   Shelly’s release marked an opportunity for middle school students to learn about terrapin ecology and the environmental impacts on the Chesapeake Bay.
*   Poplar Island was previously eroded by the Chesapeake waters, but it is now under construction with various species of wildlife calling it home.

**STORY 11: Talbot school board dives into test score trends**

*   Talbot County school officials examined state test scores for 2024-2025 Maryland Comprehensive Assessment Program, showing upward trends in math and English for some grades but dips in others.
*   The results showed increases in English proficiency for third and fourth grades, but drops for fifth and eighth grades.
*   Math proficiency spiked year-to-year for fourth, sixth, and seventh grades, but the number of students proficient in algebra fell significantly.
*   Talbot County's English proficiency dropped to 45.4%, while math proficiency was 18.9%.
*   The district aims to find solutions to address problem areas.
