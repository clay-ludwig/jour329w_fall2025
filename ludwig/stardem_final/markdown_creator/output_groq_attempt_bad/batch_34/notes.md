## Caroline County Education – Factual Notes  

### Story 3 – “Lockerman Middle School Committee previews two sites for new location” (May 14 2025)

**People / Titles / Roles**  
- **Robyn Toth** – Owner of TCA Architects; architect for the potential new Lockerman Middle School.  
- **Kevin Shearon** – Engineer for the Lockerman project.  
- **Derek Simmons** – Superintendent (of Caroline County Public Schools).  

**Schools, Institutions, Organizations**  
- **Lockerman Middle School** – Existing middle school; current site may remain a school but could be repurposed.  
- **Lockerman Middle School Committee** – Committee evaluating new locations and specifications for the school.  
- **North Caroline High School** – Potential adjacent site (behind the high school).  
- **Caroline County Sheriff's Office** – Landmark near the Double Hills Road site.  
- **Caroline County Emergency Services** – Landmark near the Double Hills Road site.  
- **Caroline County** – Owns the 76‑acre parcel that includes the Double Hills Road site; County is providing land for the project.  

**Site Details & Acreage**  
- **Site 1 (North Caroline High School location)** – 23‑acre parcel west of North Caroline High School. Contains a solar field area and a pasture connected to the Career Center.  
- **Site 2 (Double Hills Road location)** – Located near the Caroline County Sheriff’s Office and Emergency Services buildings on a 76‑acre county‑owned parcel; the committee is considering a 21‑acre portion for the school.  

**Infrastructure / Road Details**  
- Double Hills Road is a **20‑21‑foot** road with **no shoulders** on either side.  
- Proposal to add shoulders on the school‑side (or site‑side) to improve access and safety.  

**Utilities**  
- Public water and sewer could be extended from Denton’s systems to the potential new school site.  

**Dates & Timeline**  
- **May 14 2025** – First community meeting held (Thursday).  
- **June 2 2025** – Next public meeting at Lockerman Middle School, 6 p.m.  
- **July 1 2025** – Committee presentation to the Board of Education.  
- **July 2025** – Board of Education decision expected (“board's got a big decision to make in July”).  

**Quotes (Exact wording)**  
- *Robyn Toth*: “We’re going to be gathering it, reviewing it… Then as we go through the site design, we’ll be trying to incorporate as many of their concerns and try to accentuate any of the possibilities that they were saying.”  
- *Kevin Shearon*: “Double Hills is a 20‑21 foot road with no shoulders on either side… So, we would consider adding shoulders, at least on the school side or the site side for ACLD cell and just a wider feel for the right.”  
- *Derek Simmons*: “It was great to see the turnout… It was great to see the engagement, folks are really passionate about their faults, which is great. Our architects and our consultants need that information now as we’re working in the very early stages of design. I’m glad they came out.”  

**Key Issues / Themes**  
- Selection between two potential sites (behind North Caroline High vs. Double Hills Road).  
- Community input on historic value, safety, and design considerations.  
- Need for road improvements (adding shoulders).  
- Potential sharing of bus routes with North Caroline High School.  
- Utility extensions (water and sewer) for new site.

### Story 7 – “High school graduation rate reaches seven‑year high” (April 18 2025)

**Statewide Data (including Caroline County)**  
- Maryland overall high‑school graduation rate (2024): **88%** (highest since 2017).  
- **Caroline County** graduation rate (2024): **87.5%** (below state average).  
- Talbot County: 95% (for context).  
- Dorchester County: 84.4% (for context).  

**Quotes (relevant to statewide context)**  
- *Theresa Timmons Parrott* (MSDE official, multilingual education): “Last year was the largest year‑over‑year gain of any student group, and I think it’s a testimony to targeted, sustained supports that are making the difference.”  
- *Carey Wright* (State Superintendent of Schools): “When we set high standards, deliver strong instruction, and invest in proven supports, our students show that they will exceed expectations.”  

**Relevant Themes**  
- Graduation gains among historically underserved groups (Hispanic, multilingual learners).  
- Connection to the **Blueprint for Maryland’s Future** reforms (early childhood, ELL funding, college & career pathways).  
- Ongoing equity gaps, especially in rural Eastern Shore counties such as Caroline.
