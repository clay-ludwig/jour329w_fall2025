## STORY 5 – “Post Labor Day start slated for Caroline 2025‑26 school year” (Mar 12 2025)

- **Caroline County Board of Education**
  - Voted on the 2025‑2026 school‑year calendar.
  - Decided on a **post‑Labor Day start** (first day of school **Tuesday, Sept. 2, 2025**).
  - Teachers will return for planning on **Aug. 25, 2025**.
  - **Graduation dates**
    - Colonel Richardson High School – **May 27, 2026**
    - North Caroline High School – **May 28, 2026**
  - **Last day of school**
    - Students: **June 17, 2026**
    - Teachers: **June 18, 2026** (the day after)
  - **Inclement‑weather days**
    - 3 days built into the 2025‑26 calendar (4 days were used in 2024‑25).
- **Key personnel quoted / mentioned**
  - **Tara Downes** – Assistant to the Superintendent; presented the calendar at the March 4 board meeting.
  - **Chrissy Bartz** – Board Member; expressed concerns about early start and cited feedback from parents/teachers preferring a later start.
  - **Derek Simmons** – Caroline County Public Schools Superintendent; noted the board’s ability to adjust snow‑day count and to appeal to the state.
- **Survey**
  - Sent to parents and staff asking preferred start date.
  - **Result:** Pre‑Labor Day start was preferred by staff and parents, but board felt conversations indicated a preference for post‑Labor Day.
- **Author / Contact**
  - **Ahmad Garnett** – agarnett@chespub.com

## STORY 10 – “Caroline superintendent projects ‘good budget year,’ discusses Blueprint” (Dec 11 2024)

- **Key Personnel**
  - **Derek Simmons** – Superintendent, Caroline County Public Schools (CCPS)
    - Provided fiscal‑year 2026 budget outlook.
    - Discussed impact of the **Blueprint for Maryland’s Future** on funding.
  - **Troy Plutschak** – Board Member; asked about cost of external audits.
- **Budget & Finance (FY 2025 & FY 2026)**
  - FY 2025 budget:
    - **≈ 60 %** of unrestricted funds spent on **salaries and wages**.
    - **≈ 66 %** of FY 25 revenue came from the **state** (unrestricted money).
    - **Contracted services** accounted for **just under 7 %** of unrestricted budget.
  - FY 2026 outlook:
    - Dependent on outcomes of the **2024 state legislative session** (budget clarity not expected until **mid‑April**).
    - No major changes expected in **federal money** or **county allocations**.
    - Anticipated **“good budget year”** due to:
      - Increases in **multilingual learner**, **special education**, and overall **enrollment**.
  - **Audit considerations**
    - Moving audits in‑house deemed “a world of hurt”; would require additional finance staff and increase principal workload.
- **Blueprint for Maryland’s Future**
  - No major adjustments assumed for FY 26; any changes could have “detrimental impacts” on CCPS, which “is not a district that has extra wealth to go to to fill gaps.”
  - **Teacher salary mandates**
    - Minimum teacher salary of **$26,000** required by **July 2026** (CCPS has not yet met this mark).
    - Goal to raise starting teacher salary to **$60,000 by FY 27**; district is “roughly $5,000 away.”
- **State fiscal context**
  - Maryland faces a projected **$2.7 million budget deficit** for FY 2026.
- **Quotes from Derek Simmons**
  - “We are really contingent upon what happens at the state level for our funding… we will not have a clear picture of our budget … until mid‑April at best.”
  - “When we talk about where our expenses come from, it's people. No surprise as we've said for years, we are a people business. It takes people to educate children. It takes people to make school work.”
  - “We are anticipating what should be a good budget year for Caroline County Public Schools.”
  - “Not trying to paint a dire picture, but I don’t want to paint roses where they may not exist… Changes to FY 26 funding can have absolute detrimental impacts to Caroline County Public Schools.”
  - “We are still working to get our starting teacher salary up to the mandated $60,000 by FY27… We’re roughly $5,000 away.”

  - District audits are frequent; in‑house auditing would be difficult.
