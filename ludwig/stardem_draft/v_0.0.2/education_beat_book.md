## 1. Core Overview  

The Eastern Shore’s education ecosystem spans three K‑12 county districts (Talbot, Caroline, Dorchester), a regional community‑college system (Chesapeake College), and a handful of charter/technical programs.  Because the counties share a single community‑college district, decisions at the state and county level often intersect—especially around capital funding, workforce‑training initiatives, and the implementation of **Blueprint for Maryland’s Future** (BMF).  

Board of Education politics are currently fluid: recent resignations in Dorchester, a high‑profile candidate filing in Talbot, and budget debates in Caroline illustrate how governance, health, and personal ambition can reshape school leadership quickly.  

A new layer of coverage has emerged around **district‑level health‑benefits litigation**, **post‑pandemic graduation pathways**, **civic‑education competitions**, **regional capital‑project collaborations**, **technology‑discipline policies**, **board‑culture disputes**, **budget‑strain issues such as soaring lunch‑debt balances** that cut across county lines and link K‑12 schools to community‑college resources and local heritage groups.  

*Recent additions (March 2025) show two further cross‑cutting themes:*  

* **Chronic absenteeism** – now a statewide policy focus, with Mid‑Shore counties reporting rates between 22.9 % and 38.3 % (see Section 5).  
* **Celebration of standout educators** – the story of QACPS Teacher of the Year Andrea Schulte (Kent Island High School) highlights how individual programs (art, scholarships, inclusive pedagogy) can become beat‑level hooks and generate community‑wide interest.  

### New cross‑cutting theme – Student Mental‑Health & Wellness Programs (2023‑2025)  
- In October 2023, international motivational performer **Alex Boyé** toured Talbot County Public Schools (TCPS) under the “You Are a 10!” program, delivering talks on self‑worth, resilience, and mental‑health awareness to students from 5th‑12th grade.  
- The tour was a joint effort of **ParentGuidance.org**, the **Cook Center for Human Connection**, and was funded by the **MD Aware II grant**. TCPS Superintendent **Sharon Pepukayi** praised the partnership as “phenomenal” for bringing therapeutic support to a “current youth mental‑health climate.”  
- This initiative illustrates how external NGOs, state‑level grant programs, and district leadership can collaborate to address student wellness—a story vein that can be followed up with: impact assessments, future speaker series, grant‑tracking, and the role of mental‑health nonprofits in school policy.  

### New cross‑cutting theme – Green‑School & Environmental Literacy (2025)  
- Two Caroline County elementary schools—**Denton Elementary** and **Greensboro Elementary**—earned **Maryland Association for Environmental and Outdoor Education (MAEOE) Green‑School certification** in 2025.  
- Program coordinators **Julie Strange** (Denton) and **Zellie Kneller Kidigarden** (Greensboro) describe a two‑year application process that required curriculum redesign, community‑partner outreach, and hands‑on projects (e.g., breakfast‑bag recycling, garden planting, pollinator habitats).  
- Partnerships include **Pickering Creek Audubon Center**, **Chesapeake Bay Foundation**, **Tuckahoe State Park**, and **Wetlands on Wheels**. Grants secured for future tree‑planting and sustainability projects show a growing pipeline of funding for environmental education.  

### New cross‑cutting theme – Civic Literacy & Constitutional Education (2024‑2025)  
- The **Mid‑Shore Constitution Alliance** (hosted by the Constitution Alliance, the Institute for Religion, Politics and Culture at Washington College, and Operation Frederick Douglass on the Hill) staged its third annual “Our Journey to Liberty and Freedom” event at the Academy Art Museum on Sept. 17, 2024.  
- The program featured actors portraying **Frederick Douglass** and **Thomas Jefferson**, a “citizenship week” schedule, and a $1,000 higher‑education scholarship awarded to a local student.  
- State Sen. **Johnny Mautz** (R‑Caroline, Dorchester, …) participated in the dialogue, underscoring the political relevance of civic‑education initiatives.  

### New cross‑cutting theme – Immigration Enforcement & School Safety (2025)  
- In a Jan. 30, 2025 letter to families, TCPS Superintendent **Sharon Pepukayi** reaffirmed that schools remain “safe places” for all students, regardless of immigration status, after the Trump administration rescinded the 2018 “sensitive locations” policy that had limited ICE activity in schools, churches, and shelters.  
- The letter listed resources from the **American Civil Liberties Union** and the **Chesapeake Multicultural Resource Center**, signaling a growing partnership network for undocumented and immigrant families.  
- This story opens a beat‑level thread on how federal immigration policy shifts affect local school climate, student enrollment, and community trust, and how districts craft communication strategies in politically volatile moments.  

### New cross‑cutting theme – School Board Election Politics & Advocacy (2024‑2025)  
- Candidate **Karla Wieland‑Cherry** (Trappe, Talbot County) announced a run for the Talbot County Board of Education District 5, noting backlash from the Star Democrat after the paper reported her endorsement by **Moms for Liberty**. ### 2.1 Literacy & Science‑of‑Reading Initiatives  

**Maryland READS** – a statewide nonprofit launched in 2021 to combat Maryland’s “literary crisis.”  
- **Key Personnel**: Executive Director **Trish Brennan‑Gac**; member **Fred Curtis**.
