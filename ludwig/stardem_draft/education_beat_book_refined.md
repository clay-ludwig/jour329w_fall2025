# Education Beat Book – Easton Star-Democrat
## Caroline County, Maryland

This guide covers the education beat in Caroline County, Maryland. The county operates 10 public schools serving approximately 5,600 students. Caroline County Public Schools has become a focal point for debates about school performance, curriculum, and community expectations that mirror broader tensions in rural Maryland education.

## The Central Institution

Caroline County Public Schools operates under the governance of the Caroline County Board of Education, with Dr. Derek Simmons serving as superintendent after his appointment in 2022, having served as interim superintendent since March 2021. The district faces challenges common to rural Maryland systems: teacher recruitment and retention, particularly in STEM fields and special education; funding constraints tied to the state aid formula; and persistent questions about technology access following pandemic-era learning disruptions. What distinguishes Caroline County is the intensity of community scrutiny focused on school rankings and performance metrics.

Current enrollment is approximately 5,608 students, down about 40 students from last year. Elementary enrollment saw an increase of 53 students, while middle schools had a reduction of 51 and high schools a reduction of 61. Home school enrollment has increased, with 537 students this year, up from 466 in 2022-2023. Simmons repeatedly emphasizes consistency around evidence-based approaches in curriculum and instruction, a theme that shapes district policy from textbook adoption to professional development.

Amy Towers serves as Supervisor of Instruction, overseeing curriculum, instruction, and professional development across the district. She played a lead role in the 2023 AP Physics textbook adoption. William Gray, appointed in August 2025, serves as Supervisor of Instruction for Physical Education, leading PE curriculum, staffing, and athletic facility policy. Elizabeth Saylor oversees early learning initiatives as Supervisor of Instruction for the Judy Center. Lindsey McCormick serves as Director of College and Career Readiness, coordinating AP programs, PSAT and SAT testing, dual enrollment, and post-secondary counseling. She also coordinates technical education pathways and partnerships with local employers, including the annual Youth Career Exploration Fair organized with the Rotary Club of Denton. Kirk Howie serves as Director of Student Services. Bill Mengel serves as Director of Operations and becomes a critical source for facilities planning and construction projects.

The Board of Education makes policy and budget decisions that shape the district's direction. Michele Weaver serves as Board President as of December 2024. Chrissy Bartz and Stefanie Johnson were sworn in as new board members in December 2024. Donna DiGiacomo, a retired teacher, also serves on the board. Troy Plutschak is a board member. Mark Jones served as Vice President during an earlier period, and Rick Barton's term as Board President ended in December 2024.

The board approved Simmons' request in December 2023 to move the final budget proposal deadline from February to April, acknowledging that state revenue projections are not finalized until the General Assembly completes its work in April. This shift gives district staff additional time to align Blueprint for Maryland's Future mandates with actual state funding levels.

## The Rankings Question

SchoolDigger ranks Caroline County Public Schools 16th of 25 Maryland school districts. The rankings matter because they increasingly drive policy decisions. The Board of Education and superintendent cite them when justifying budget requests and curriculum reforms. Real estate agents report that prospective buyers ask about school quality, and there is concern that rankings influence family relocation decisions.

Superintendent Simmons recognizes inherent challenges with the current system, noting that the report card does not adequately recognize student growth and serves as too much of a proxy for poverty. Caroline County has 100 percent of schools at the three-star rating or above, with all middle and high schools receiving three stars. Lockerman Middle School jumped to three stars, while Greensboro Elementary fell from four to three stars. Preston Elementary received four stars and ranks in the 67th percentile statewide.

Maryland is currently evaluating the accountability system. The committee is building on the Maryland Assessment and Accountability Task Force's recommendations to support implementation of the school accountability system. The recommendations were presented to the State Board of Education in December 2024. Since June 2025, the committee has met monthly to develop proposed next steps for presentation to the State Board by the end of the year.

Several story angles emerge from the rankings debate. A trend analysis tracking Caroline's position over five years would reveal whether the district is improving or stagnating, and which specific metrics drive changes in overall rank. An investigation into real estate impacts could survey local agents and examine home price data to assess whether school quality affects property values.

Most important would be an equity analysis disaggregating ranking data by race, socioeconomic status, and geography within Caroline County to uncover achievement gaps that overall rankings might obscure. The district's data office and the Maryland State Department of Education can provide the underlying numbers that feed into the rankings.

## Blueprint for Maryland's Future

The Blueprint for Maryland's Future, a comprehensive education reform law passed in 2021, sets statewide targets for teacher pay, early learning, school construction, and equity. At the Maryland Association of Counties Winter Conference in December 2024, Superintendent Simmons emphasized that "something written three years ago isn't perfect today" and called for "local flexibility and understanding of each district's context." His remarks signal that Caroline County is actively reviewing Blueprint mandates and may seek adjustments that fit the county's enrollment size, tax base, and rural character.

Under the Blueprint, all Maryland teachers will earn a salary of at least $60,000 beginning in 2026. Starting teacher salaries in Caroline County for next year are over $55,000, and the district will reach the $60,000 salary minimum by 2026 for teachers on the career ladder. Stories should examine the district's recruitment pipeline, including partnerships with teacher-preparation programs, salary competitiveness with neighboring districts, and retention rates by subject area and school. Special education and STEM positions typically prove hardest to fill.

The Blueprint's funding requirements may strain local budgets. Reporters should track Caroline County budget proposals to see how Blueprint mandates for teacher salaries, pre-K expansion, and school facility upgrades are being funded. Any formal requests by the district or Board of Education for flexibility in implementing Blueprint requirements deserve coverage, as they reflect tensions between state mandates and local fiscal capacity.

## Facilities and Capital Projects

The district is planning a new middle school to replace Lockerman Middle School, with shovels in the ground projected by as early as July 2027, and move-in for the start of school in August 2029. The anticipated price tag is $86 million. The Caroline County Board of Education unanimously voted in August 2025 to locate the new Lockerman Middle School on the North Caroline High School campus in Ridgely. The North Caroline High site will allow 15.52 acres of space for the build of Lockerman Middle.

Bill Mengel, director of operations for Caroline County Public Schools, serves as the key point person for facilities planning. Robyn Toth serves as project architect, handling design and site-selection issues. The board is entering into a memorandum of understanding with the Maryland Stadium Authority to provide guidance and support for the Lockerman Middle School build. The only concern with the North Caroline High site is the solar array on the property. The county has been in conversation with Tesla to purchase the solar array.

Lockerman Middle School originated as an all-Black high school and has gone through several iterations to becoming the current middle school that serves the central and northern areas of Caroline County. The oldest part of the building that exists today was erected in 1963, but educational activities have taken place on the site since the 1930s. The Lockerman Schools Association Inc., chaired by Willie Woods, preserves this legacy, sponsors annual events, and awards 13 scholarships each year to Caroline County students pursuing higher education. This heritage adds depth to coverage of the new building project and connects current facilities planning to the county's civil rights history.

A Lockerman Middle School construction series could track the project from planning through completion, examining design decisions, community input, construction progress, budget adherence, and the educational philosophy behind co-locating middle and high school facilities. Travis Breeding, president of the Caroline County Commissioners, has requested regular updates on the middle school feasibility study, signaling that county-level approval and funding coordination will be critical to the project's success.

## Individual Schools and Leadership

North Caroline High School is led by Principal Matt Spiker and serves as the second-largest high school in the county. The school will share its campus with the new Lockerman Middle School. Colonel Richardson High School is led by Principal Nikki VonDenBosch and hosts growing STEM programs.

Denton Elementary School is led by Principal Dr. Sarah Crebs-Oberdorf, who earned her doctorate in January 2024. Her research focused on race and reading instruction, and she previously served as a special education teacher and assistant principal at Ridgely Elementary. Lindsay Grow serves as assistant principal. First-day-of-school coverage in September 2025 at Denton Elementary highlighted enthusiastic student arrivals and bus logistics, underscoring the importance of reporters arriving on-site early to capture the energy and operational details of school openings. Denton Elementary received Green School certification in 2025.

Greensboro Elementary School has been certified as a Green School since 2021, with program director Zellie Kneller Kidigarden overseeing environmental literacy efforts. The school's star rating fell from four to three stars in recent rankings.

The Federalsburg Judy Center Early Learning Hub, located at Federalsburg Elementary School, celebrated 25 years of operation in August 2025. Heather Harding serves as coordinator, with Elizabeth Saylor providing oversight as Supervisor of Instruction for early learning. The center provides school-readiness services for children ages five and under and their families, representing an important component of the district's early childhood education infrastructure.

Danielle Dennis was named Caroline County Teacher of the Year for 2024-25 and presented her journey to the Board in August 2025. She represents a potential source for stories on instructional excellence and teacher leadership.

## Cell Phone Management and Technology Policy

Caroline County launched a pilot program in 2024 using magnetic lock pouches from Yondr to restrict student phone use during school hours. This approach reflects broader national debates about smartphone distraction, mental health impacts, and classroom management. The pilot's results will shape whether the district expands the policy systemwide and offers a case study for examining the practical challenges of implementing phone restrictions.

Beyond phone management, technology equity deserves ongoing scrutiny. The district has worked to expand one-to-one device programs, but gaps in home internet access persist, particularly in rural areas.

## Community Partners and Support Organizations

The Caroline County Public Library supports literacy initiatives, summer reading programs, and adult education. The library participates in One Maryland One Book and other statewide programs that connect to school curriculum. In August 2025, the library received a $1,450 donation from the Imagination Library program, reflecting ongoing community support for early literacy. The library also operates a Read to Me therapy-dog literacy program that pairs children struggling to read with certified therapy dogs and handlers. The program operates out of the library and makes classroom visits when permitted by schools.

Chesapeake College maintains an Eastern Shore campus with LaSella Hall serving as DEI Director. The college offers dual-enrollment opportunities and workforce training that support college-access equity initiatives for Caroline County students. The college operates a Hispanic/Latino Student Union and other programs that serve Caroline students. Larry Porter, Vice President of the Caroline County Commission, serves as a key decision-maker for local funding of regional post-secondary projects.

The Rotary Club of Denton sponsors the annual Youth Career Exploration Fair for CCPS 10th-graders, with Sue Simmons serving as event chair. The fair provides venue space, business-mentor networks, and community-service funding that connect students to regional career pathways. The Upper Shore Workforce Investment Board, with Youth Outreach Coordinator Deborah Boulden, provides career-readiness workshops and connects students to regional apprenticeship pipelines.

Parent and community groups including Moms for Liberty local chapters and PTA leaders frequently host debates, advocacy events, and community outreach. These groups provide important perspectives on curriculum debates, school policy, and parent engagement.

The Caroline County Historical Society partners with schools on local-history programming and public lectures that connect students to community heritage, including a November 2023 presentation on Anna Douglass.

## Ongoing Coverage Priorities

The state funding formula for education undergoes periodic revision in Annapolis, and changes could significantly affect Caroline County's budget. Reporters should monitor relevant legislative hearings, track local lobbying efforts, and analyze how formula changes would affect per-pupil spending. The Lockerman Middle School construction project adds urgency to fiscal analysis.

Budget cycles provide regular story opportunities. The annual budget process reveals priorities, trade-offs, and community tensions. Bond measures for school construction or renovation generate particularly intense coverage, as they require voter approval and affect tax rates.

Student achievement data released by the Maryland State Department of Education offers regular benchmarks for assessing district performance. Reporters should obtain and analyze test scores, graduation rates, college enrollment rates, and other metrics, always disaggregating data to identify disparities by race, income, and geography.

Student success stories provide necessary balance to coverage focused on rankings and challenges. Caroline County students who earn state scholarships, win STEM competitions, gain admission to selective colleges, or achieve other recognitions deserve profiles that showcase what the district does well.

## Building Sources

The superintendent and board chair are essential sources who should be cultivated for regular background conversations, not just formal interviews. Individual board members often have distinct perspectives and areas of expertise worth developing. The district's communications director or public information officer can facilitate access and provide data, but should not be the only voice representing the district.

Bill Mengel becomes a critical source for facilities planning, construction projects, and operational challenges. Amy Towers can speak to curriculum initiatives, professional development, and instructional policy. William Gray offers expertise on physical education and athletic programs. Elizabeth Saylor provides insight into early learning initiatives. Lindsey McCormick can explain college readiness programs, AP expansion, post-secondary outcomes, and career-technical partnerships. Kirk Howie can speak to student services issues. School principals offer ground-level perspectives on how policies play out in practice. The president of the teachers' union or association can speak to workforce issues and teacher concerns. PTA leaders at individual schools provide parent perspectives.

Willie Woods can provide historical context and community perspective on the new middle school project and the broader legacy of Black education in Caroline County. Travis Breeding and other Caroline County Commissioners influence capital funding decisions that affect school construction and major projects. Caroline County's representatives in the Maryland General Assembly influence education funding and policy. The Maryland State Department of Education provides data, implements state policy, and sometimes intervenes in local districts.

Real estate professionals, economic development officials, and business leaders can speak to how school quality affects the county's economic competitiveness and ability to attract families and employers.

## The Reporter's Approach

This beat requires patience, attention to detail, and a commitment to covering both problems and solutions. The community cares deeply about its schools, and coverage should reflect that seriousness while maintaining fairness and accuracy. The rankings debate provides a ready frame for many stories, but reporters should resist letting a single metric dominate coverage at the expense of deeper questions about equity, opportunity, and what students actually learn.

Education coverage works best when it balances accountability journalism with explanatory reporting that helps readers understand complex systems and policy debates. The best stories often emerge from sustained attention to an issue over time rather than reactive coverage of individual events. Building trust with sources across the political spectrum allows reporters to understand tensions and trade-offs that shape decision-making.

First-day-of-school coverage requires early arrival to capture bus logistics, student arrivals, and the energy of a new academic year. Annual events like spelling bees, graduation ceremonies, and budget hearings provide regular story opportunities that should be covered consistently to track trends over time.

The goal is not to advocate for any particular policy outcome but to provide the information communities need to make informed decisions about their schools. That means asking hard questions of people in power, listening carefully to parents and teachers, and always keeping student outcomes at the center of the story.
